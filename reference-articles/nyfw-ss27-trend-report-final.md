I'm late, but late is better than quick trends. And this is a lot of hell of work. So let's go through the real findings that have come up in my data, broken down by material, fit, silhouette, and commentary in between. Because I'm sick of trend reports based on nothing. My job is to separate what's real from what's not, so you don't waste your time.

## Methodology

A quick note before the insights. I tagged the 13* biggest shows in New York Fashion Week Spring Summer 2027, and every look of the collection has been tagged against a fixed taxonomy (with a tool I built called "tagging tool") by garment type, fit, fabric, colour, and detail. And each trend is reported in two ways: its share within a brand's own collection and its share across the whole NYFW SS27 season. Where it's useful, I've also double checked consumer searches for the specific trends, and complemented it with what the press is saying about a trend.

## Materials

### Silk is the season's real material story

This chart shows how many of the brands put that specific material on the runway at least once. Silk showed up at every one of the catwalks, most often as satin, sometimes as chiffon. Wool was the second most consistent material, worth breaking it down by weave; crepe was the dominant type. Sheer touched most houses too, and cotton showed up almost everywhere, as well as knitwear and leather.

Ok, ok… silk is the most dominant, but in what shape, what form, what is made of silk? It's worth being specific about what "silk" actually looks like (two key things):

- Silk slip dress. It's the second most common silhouette in this data (shown below). Conner Ives does fluid bias-cut slip dresses, Calvin Klein reworking the shape into evening gowns. And huge trend.
- Silk cummerbunds reworked as a waist-defining accessory or belt, and Conner Ives gets called out specifically for pairing a silk cummerbund with a silky fish-print dress.

## Silhouettes

### We are all obsessed with the midi skirt

This chart shows how many of the brands put that specific silhouette on the runway at least once. The midi skirt reached every single house, the only silhouette this season with no gaps at all. And a HUGE one across the rest of London and Milan Fashion Weeks, outside this dataset entirely. Not to mention Prada's obsession with the knee length skirt: an entire 63 look show titled simply "The Skirt".

The column midi skirt specifically was flagged as one of the season's more directional shapes.

### The slip dress and midi skirt comparison: which one wins?

The midi skirt was the more universal silhouette on the runway, in every single house, but slip dresses were also among the top trends spotted. And when I look at the actual search data, slip dress runs higher across the majority of the past twelve months. The runway data and the search data are telling two different stories here, and that gap is the real finding: slip dress reads as the settled, dependable one, a silhouette that has already proven its staying power across a full year of consistent searching, while midi skirt is the one I'd bet on building towards its own.

Slip dress runs higher than midi skirt in 50 of the last 53 weeks. Outside the show-season spike (Feb to May), slip dress averages 25 against midi skirt's 9, nearly three times the search interest, even though midi skirt was the more universal silhouette on the actual runways.

### A few smaller silhouettes worth flagging

None big enough for the chart above, but each reaching enough different houses to be more than one designer's idea. Denim is a biiiiig one of these; it was present in 38% of the catwalks (most present in Ralph Lauren, Tommy Hilfiger, Calvin Klein, Conner Ives or Thom Browne), mostly as a jacket but also showing up as trousers and a coat, making its case as more than trouser fabric. The same story showed up in my [Fall Trends Autopsy](https://thedatafashionbrief.substack.com/p/fall-trends-autopsy). People are really leaning into all things denim right now, especially relaxed trousers.

## Colour breakdown

### Navy and brown lead, but red sits closer to the surface than it looks

Navy leads the season's colour weight at 16%, and (of course) brown close behind at 14%, then gold/vibrant yellow at 13% (evolution of the butter yellow that kept showing up all summer). Deep green or dusty blue is showing as pops of colour now that summer's left us, a more grown-up take on the eclectic turquoise and green shades that defined the summer season. Red comes in at 9%, the season's seventh biggest colour.

The press is obsessed with it too. [Marie Claire ran a whole piece](https://www.marieclaire.com/fashion/new-york-fashion-week-street-style-trend-spring-summer-2027-red-color/) on red taking over NYFW SS27 street style, and named Tommy Hilfiger and Tory Burch specifically among the houses pushing it. I've been saying colour is trending for a while now, and [here](https://thedatafashionbrief.substack.com/p/why-is-everyone-obsessed-with-colour) I explain why.

But [WWD's own write-up of Pantone's NYFW colour report](https://wwd.com/fashion-news/fashion-trends/pantone-nyfw-spring-2027-color-trends-1239203317/) leads with boldness and saturated primaries, vibrant red and cobalt blue specifically. The data backs that up directly: Tommy Hilfiger builds 16% of its own palette around red, nearly double the 9% season figure, with Conner Ives at 12% and Altuzarra at 10% not far behind.

## The trend matrix: classifying brands by trend alignment and colour intensity

The matrix below classifies brands based on how trend-forward and colour-intense a brand has been at New York Fashion Week SS27.

How to read the matrix is simple: alignment averages a house's own share of every trend this piece has found real, materials and silhouettes together into one number. A house near the top built a real chunk of its collection around what the season was doing, a house near the bottom stayed more independent from the spotted trends. Say a brand sat at 20% silk, 15% wide-leg trousers, and 10% midi skirts; its alignment score is the average of those three, 15%. Colour intensity is a separate number: how much of that palette leans into colour rather than neutrals.

Coach scores highest on alignment, the biggest share of its own collection built around the season's real silhouette trends, wide-leg trouser, midi skirt, slip dress and the smaller silhouettes covered above, with Ralph Lauren, Khaite and Tory Burch close behind. Khaite lands high on alignment and low on colour at once, a house that's clearly tracking the season's real shapes without matching its colour story, exactly the kind of case a single-axis ranking would have missed. Tommy Hilfiger and Ulla Johnson are the two houses over-indexing on both axes, the majority of their SS27 looks track the trends this piece identifies as real, and their palettes lean colour-forward rather than neutral. Michael Kors sits lowest on alignment, with Thom Browne and Calvin Klein close to it, three houses running the most independent collections on shape, and neutral on colour too.

## The Takeaway

What NYFW SS27 was actually made of:

- Silk is the season's material, present at every decoded house, most often as satin. Wool's real story is crepe specifically, not wool in general.
- The midi skirt is the season's universal silhouette, present at every house, even though wide-leg trousers are the bigger number by volume.
- Coach, Ralph Lauren, Khaite and Tory Burch scored highest on alignment, a real, meaningful share of their own collections built around the season's actual named silhouette trends. Michael Kors, Thom Browne and Calvin Klein ran the most independent collections.
- Tommy Hilfiger and Ulla Johnson are the only two houses over-indexing on both alignment and colour at once, tracking the season's real trends while also leaning hard into colour. Khaite does the opposite, high alignment with almost no colour at all, proof the two axes genuinely measure different things.
