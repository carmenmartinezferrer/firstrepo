# Outreach Pitches, Draft 1

## Part 1: the media concept

### The idea in one line

The Data Fashion Brief for [Outlet] is a recurring, branded data column where I take one trend, house or colour each month and follow it all the way through, from the runway to what people actually search for and, finally, to what people are actually wearing on the street.

### The four formats

1. **The Trend Trace.** One trend followed end to end: how many runways showed it, how search interest moved over the year, and whether it made it onto the street, so readers get a straight answer to "is this a real trend or a moment?"
2. **The Business Case.** One brand or house decoded through data, for example why a label is suddenly climbing, what its collection is actually built around, and whether its customers are following.
3. **The Colour Debrief.** The season's real palette, built from tagging every look, with the houses that over-index on each colour and a check against what the press is calling the colour of the season.
4. **Ground Data.** The part nobody else does: I go out with the same taxonomy I use on the runway, tag what people are actually wearing on a set street at a set time, and interview them about why, so the piece ends with real street numbers and real voices next to the runway and search data.

Ground Data can run on its own or close every one of the other three formats, which is what makes the series feel like a full investigation rather than a chart dump.

### What to attach or link

- A one-page concept sheet (the four formats above, with one sample chart each).
- Your two or three strongest published pieces, ideally the NYFW SS27 Trend Report and the FashionUnited piece.
- Your audience snapshot: subscribers, followers, and the US and UK split.
- One sample Ground Data idea that is specific to their city or readers (for US outlets that means a New York street check, which you could do during NYFW in February).

### Who to send it to first

US titles first, since that's where most of your audience is: Vogue Business, Who What Wear, Harper's Bazaar US, The Cut, Glossy and Fashionista, then UK: Grazia UK (new editor this month), Stylist and TheIndustry.fashion. Big titles like Vogue usually commission from section editors rather than a general inbox, so find the fashion features or fashion news editor on LinkedIn or the masthead and pitch them directly.

## Part 2: the brand partnership pitch

### Brand pitch email

Subject: The Data Fashion Brief x [Brand]: what the data says about [their product or category]

Hi [first name],

I'm Carmen, the analyst behind The Data Fashion Brief, where I decode fashion trends with real runway and search data for a readership that's mostly in the US and UK and works in or around the industry.

[One specific, true, sourced line about the brand that connects to your data, for example a trend they own, a category your data shows is rising, or a recent index ranking.]

I'd love to build a piece around that with you, [pick one: a sponsored data drop on the trend they own / a Trend Autopsy featuring their pieces as the shoppable picks / a Reel and Substack feature pairing my data with their collection / a talk at one of their events].

I'll send my media kit with audience numbers and past work, and I'm happy to jump on a quick call.

Best,
Carmen

### What to include in the media kit

- **Who you are:** two lines on your background (Inditex, YNAP, a senior data analyst role) and what DFB does.
- **Audience:** Substack subscribers and open rate, Instagram and TikTok followers, the US and UK split, age range, and engagement rate. Take every number from your own analytics on the day you send it.
- **What makes you different:** you're the only one on their list bringing real runway and search data, so lead with a chart.
- **Formats you offer:** sponsored data drop, Trend Autopsy feature, Reel, Substack sponsorship slot, event talk or panel.
- **Proof:** published pieces, FashionUnited, fashion week invitations, and any past brand work.
- **Rates:** either a rate card or "rates on request", but know your floor before the call.

### Two things to check before you send

- **Your Farfetch contract.** Most employment contracts have an outside-work or conflict-of-interest clause, and luxury brands are Farfetch partners, so check whether paid brand partnerships need approval first. Never use anything from your day job's internal data in a pitch or piece.
- **Disclosure.** Paid or gifted collaborations need clear ad labelling in both the US (FTC) and UK (ASA and CMA).

## Part 3: the pink pitches (Revolve and Charlotte Simone)

All the numbers below are from the Paris SS27 Colour Index (16 houses, 997 looks). Pink is 5% of the season palette and is in 8% of all looks, at 13 of the 16 houses, with ballet pink as the shade that connects the most houses. Purple is 1% of the palette and 3% of looks, at five houses, and red is 2% of the palette and 4% of looks.
