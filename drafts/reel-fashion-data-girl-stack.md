Not built from an article this time, so there are no stats to protect, but every tool and habit below comes from what is actually in the repo and the skills (Vogue Runway screenshots, the tagging spreadsheet, Google Trends CSVs, Canva, the Python donuts, Claude Code, Substack, Metricool, Notion, the newsletter inbox). Two angles, pick one or mix. Version A is the saveable one ("here is the stack, copy it"). Version B is the comment-bait one ("this is me"). Both run about 50 seconds, which is where your Paris Reels landed.

## Version A: "10 things you need to decode fashion week like a data analyst"

**Angle:** people think decoding fashion week means watching the shows and having taste, when it is actually ten very unglamorous tools and one spreadsheet.
**Hook (on-screen, frame 1):** THE FASHION DATA GIRL STACK (10 THINGS)

**Inicio**

Everyone asks how I decode fashion week, and the honest answer is that it is not taste, it is ten very unglamorous things and a spreadsheet. Here is the whole stack.

**Nudo** (climbing from the obvious to the one nobody expects)

1. **The Vogue Runway app.** Every look of every show, and I screenshot all of them, in runway order, with the header on so I know which brand I am looking at at 1am. [CHECK: 1am]
2. **A spreadsheet.** Not a dashboard, a spreadsheet. One row per look: colour, silhouette, material, detail. Paris alone was 997 rows.
3. **Google Trends.** Free, and the thing that separates a trend from a vibe. I download the CSV and plot every candidate on the same 0 to 100 scale, head to head, because a trend that looks strong alone can barely register next to another one.
4. **An inbox that reads like a trade paper.** BoF, Vogue Business, WWD, before I have had coffee, so I know if what I am counting matches what the industry is actually saying. [CHECK: coffee]
5. **Python, for the donuts.** Yes, the pink and black colour palette donuts are code, largest slice at twelve o'clock, every time, because I made it a rule.
6. **Canva.** Where the charts and the cut-outs become a carousel, and where I lose an hour aligning swatches. [CHECK: the hour]
7. **A Pantone-ish eye and a colour picker.** Every hex code on my swatch cards is sampled from the actual photo, not guessed.
8. **Claude Code.** My co-analyst. It builds the charts, it knows I am not allowed to use em dashes, and it has been told more than once that 44% is not a chart title.
9. **Substack and Metricool.** One for the long version, one so the Reel posts while I am at my day job as a data analyst.
10. **A very strong opinion about the word trend.** Because the whole account exists to stop people calling everything one.

**Desenlace**

So no, it is not taste, it is a thousand rows and a free Google tool. If you want the full Paris breakdown that came out of this stack, comment PARIS and I will send you the link. [CHECK: which article / keyword you want to push]

## Why the items sit in this order (the peak curve)

Both versions open on the two most recognisable tools (the app, the spreadsheet) so the viewer knows the territory, put the one real analytical idea in the middle (Google Trends on the same scale, which is the actual DFB method and the line a brand person would screenshot), and save the two surprises for the end: the AI with a written no-em-dash rule, and either the strong opinion about the word trend (A) or the shopping confession (B). The confession is the highest peak in B because it is the only line that is about you rather than about tools, and it is the one people will reply to.

## Lines I considered and cut

- "SQL at the day job." Only one mention anywhere in the repo, so I could not confirm it is yours. Add it back if true, it is very relatable for the analyst crowd.
- "Trendalytics, Lyst, Launchmetrics." They show up as sources in your articles, not as tools you open yourself, so they read as name-dropping rather than stack.
- Anything about weekends or nights beyond the single 1am joke. If you do tag on Sunday nights, say so and it becomes the most relatable line in the Reel.
