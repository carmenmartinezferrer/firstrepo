# My Learnings From My First Fashion Week Season (From the McQueen After Party to the Stella Show I Skipped)

## Everything you need to know if you're in fashion and wondering whether fashion week could happen for you too

I started posting content around nine months ago, and this season I got invited to loads of shows, from emerging designers to some not so emerging ones, plus Moschino in Milan and Stella McCartney in Paris. Unfortunately I didn't make it to either Milan or Paris, but just getting invited was an "OMG, what?" moment, followed by reading the email a couple of times to make sure it was actually meant for me.

This was all totally new for me. My following has grown a lot in the past few months, and I'm still getting used to this "second life" (or side hustle, even though it doesn't really feel like a side thing anymore), because I also have a 9 to 5 as a data analyst at a major fashion marketplace. So this one isn't about what was on the runway (that's coming in the data pieces, don't worry), it's more about what it was actually like, and what I'd tell anyone a few months into posting who's wondering if any of this can happen to them.

I had no idea how any of this worked, so I want to take you with me through all of it, every step, what I went through, and answer all your questions along the way.

And the thing I didn't expect at all is that these events are where you actually meet people, the shows are amazing to watch but you barely talk to anyone, while at a breakfast or a party you end up chatting to PRs, creators and designers who you then keep bumping into for the rest of the week.

## Milan and Paris, which I wasn't even planning to go to

Everything outside London was inbound. I wasn't planning to go at all, so I hadn't planned anything, and somehow the invites came anyway.

From my experience, a lot of things happen last minute, everything is a bit chaotic, and PR teams work veeery hard to make everything go as smoothly as possible.

The Moschino invite came the same week as the show, and I just couldn't sort everything out in a day, it would have felt forced, and from what I've seen not that many people make the trip to Milan. If I had to rank the fashion weeks by popularity, I'd say Paris, then New York and Milan (maybe these two the other way round), then Copenhagen, then London...? Thoughts?

Then Paris. Last minute, I got an email from the PR team at Stella McCartney and I managed to get invited to the show the day before. I wish I could say I went! It was that fine line between "I have to go, it's going to be amazing" and "this is a bit much", because loads of people I'd met were going to be there, I'd have had a full day, and it's a day trip from London, so it should have been fine. But the train tickets were, of course, in the clouds, it would have been disruptive with work, and I waited until the very last minute to decide. In the end I thought, if I made it here with a small following and only a few months of posting, I'll make it next time.

So until next time, Stella!

## What I Learned

1. Posting about fashion week is why Milan and Paris ended up in my inbox at all, I didn't even email them! The coverage, the tagging and the posts that went viral all paid off later, and I can only imagine how much more would have come if I'd done outreach for those too, so I'm very happy about it.
2. You don't need a huge following to get invited, I'm a few months in and still got invites I didn't think were meant for me yet.
3. If you don't get the show, try to go to the showroom, the after party, or anything really! It's so cool to experience and to meet people, and missing McQueen and ending up at the after party was one of my favourite nights of the whole season.
4. Plan for the invites you're not expecting, because my Paris and Milan problem wasn't the invite, it was that I had no budget or days off set aside, so the train prices decided for me.
5. Having a full time job and doing this on the side is a lot, so be honest with yourself about how much stress you can take, and it's ok to say no to things.
6. Doing something that's yours is addictive, and that's what keeps me going when it gets mad.

## Would I do it all again?

Yes, 100%, but a lot more organised this time! Nine months ago I would have laughed if you'd told me I'd be turning down a Stella McCartney show because of train prices, and I'm still a bit in shock that I get to write that sentence.

Have you ever said no to something you'd been dreaming about because the timing just wasn't right? Or are you a few months into posting and wondering if this could happen to you? Tell me in the comments, I read you.
