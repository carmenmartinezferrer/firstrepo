## Reel 1: Maximalism vs quiet luxury (about 60s)

**Hook (on-screen, frame 1):** MAXIMALISM REPLACED QUIET LUXURY? I CHECKED

Everyone's saying maximalism is replacing quiet luxury, so I checked my data.

And even if 56% of Paris was still neutral, colour snuck in everywhere. Pink showed up at 13 of the 16 houses, red in 4% of all looks, green and yellow as accents…

Because maximalism is basically matching things that were never supposed to match. Print on print, different textures, materials… that combination you look at and think it's great, "how is this working?". Dries and Miu Miu did it the most.

And we had a looot of it this season. A third of every look in Paris had ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading or embroidery, at every single house. Dior did it in 70% of its looks, Chloé 69%, Isabel Marant 60%.

But we also had the opposite. Lanvin, Celine and Balenciaga stayed almost completely neutral, Lanvin 89%, Celine 81%. Same quiet base, just without the volume.

So quiet luxury or maximalism? Paris said both: a quiet wardrobe wearing a lot of jewellery. I decoded every house in the full Paris report, so comment PARIS and I'll send you the link, and tell me which one you are!

## Reel 3: Paris Fashion Week, backed by data (about 60s)

What if I told you the biggest trends of Paris Fashion Week, backed by actual data? I tagged all 997 looks, and spoiler, Paris didn't get louder, it got more decorated.

First, the base. Paris was a suiting city, with wool suiting in a quarter of all looks and silk right behind it, and the shape was the column, long and straight, in over a third of everything.

Number one, the pencil skirt. Every single house showed one, mostly at the knee, and Chanel put one in half of its looks!

Number two, pink. Not loud, just everywhere, at 13 of the 16 houses, and ballet pink was the shade.

Number three, the one nobody's talking about, lingerie on top. Bandeaus, corsets, bodysuits, bras, small on their own, but put together they're 12% of all looks, as big as the wide-leg trouser.

## Reel 4: The pencil skirt, checked (about 50s)

**Hook (on-screen, frame 1):** THE PENCIL SKIRT IS BACK? I CHECKED

Everyone's saying the pencil skirt is back, so I tagged every single look from Paris Fashion Week to see what actually walked down the runway. First the base, because wool suiting showed up in a quarter of all looks, and the shape of the season was the column, in over a third of everything, so no volume and no big shoulders, just long and lean. Then the surprise I didn't see coming, which is that bras, bustiers and corset tops showed up at 14 of the 16 houses, so the lingerie top is a much bigger story than any one show makes it look. And yes, the pencil skirt was at every single house, all 16, and Paris picked the knee over the midi! But here's the catch, because Chanel, Dries Van Noten, Balmain and Valentino make up almost two thirds of those pencil skirts, and Chanel put one in half its looks. So it's on every runway, but it really belongs to four houses. If you're buying one thing from Paris, make it a knee-length pencil skirt in something soft like silk or satin.
