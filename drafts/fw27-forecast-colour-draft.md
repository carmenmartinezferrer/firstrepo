# Stop Calling Everything a Trend: Fall/Winter 2027 Colour Edition

## Two forecasters land on the same four colours for next autumn, the search data backs two of them, and the Paris runway mostly backs one

The ULTIMATE forecast FW 2027 is here!! I scraped the internet, social media, the press, and crunched the numbers to give you the twelve colours for next autumn, split into three moods. So this piece is about why those three moods exist at all, which of the twelve the forecasters agree on, which ones people are actually searching for, and which ones the [Paris SS27 runway](https://thedatafashionbrief.substack.com/p/stop-calling-everything-a-trend-the-5fd), the one I tagged myself, can already see.

## Methodology, the short version

The twelve colours come from the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast for Fall/Winter 2027, four for each of its three aesthetics, Heritage Craft, Sensory Riot and Veiled Performance. Each colour arrives with one number, and the numbers come in three different kinds, so I've turned them all into percentages a reader can actually compare: the colours with a search volume are shown as their share of the combined searches, the colours with a growth figure as % change on last year, and I've added my own Google Trends pulls where they fill a gap. Then I checked the twelve against two outside sources: the WGSN and Coloro key colours for the same season, and my own Paris SS27 colour tagging, 16 houses and 997 looks, where every look is split across up to three colours by how much of the outfit each covers. Full detail at the bottom.

## The three palettes, and why they're called that

### Heritage Craft: quiet luxury grows up, and for winter it comes in deep colour

[CHART: 06-palette-heritage-craft.png, the four Heritage Craft colours, russet red, plum purple, forest green and chestnut brown]

Stealth wealth is evolving into heritage craft. Shoppers are moving from logos to natural fabrics, with cotton up 90% and cashmere up 80% in Google searches this year against last, because what people want is a timeless wardrobe of tried and true luxury, clothes that work as an enduring staple rather than a season's statement. My Paris SS27 tagging sees the foundation of it already: wool suiting was in 25% of all looks, the biggest material of the season. The craft side shows in jacquard jackets (up 61% in searches on last year), lace (4% of all Paris looks), shrunken blazers and peplum jackets (up 24% in Google searches over the last three months) and the equestrian tall boot look, with harness boots up 476% this year. Autumn leaves and riding boots, basically, and the palette is exactly that.

The colours, in my own Google searches: **plum purple** is the one moving, up 60% (and up 154% on TikTok), which makes it the safest bet of the four, with **dark red** and **forest green** both up 50% behind it and **chestnut brown** barely moving at 10%. The forecast's own numbers tell the same story from a different angle, plum up 39% on last year, while forest green and dark red are the two least searched of the five colours it gives a volume to, 14% and 9% of the combined searches. Brown is everywhere on TikTok and nowhere in search, which is the difference between a colour people watch and a colour people go looking for.

### Sensory Riot: dressing in things you can actually feel

[CHART: 06-palette-sensory-riot.png, the four Sensory Riot colours, chartreuse, fuchsia, lilac and cobalt blue]

This is the maximalist palette, and the reasoning behind it is three things at once. Shoppers tired of generic AI imagery are turning to tactile, layered, clashing-print dressing that a machine can't replicate, years of quiet luxury have dissolved into corporate sameness, and effort is back, clothes that visibly took time to make. My data agrees on the direction: 31% of the Paris SS27 looks I tagged carried embellishment (ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading, embroidery, feathers or appliqué), at every one of the 16 houses, and the fringe skirt is the clearest signal, up 71% in searches on last year and the biggest mover among the items the forecast measured that way. Minimalism isn't over though, 56% of the Paris colour weight was still neutral, so the loud side grows while the quiet side stays.

The colours: **lilac** and **fuchsia** are the two most searched colours in the whole forecast after marigold, 25% and 21% of the combined searches, and the fuchsia end of pink is no surprise to anyone here, because I already told you [pink is back](https://thedatafashionbrief.substack.com/p/is-pink-actually-back-or-just-another), pink was in 8% of Paris looks and present at 81% of the shows I analysed, and blush pink searches are up 20% over the last three months. **Cobalt blue** is the quiet climber, up 14% on last year in the forecast and up 33% for blue over the last three months in my own pull, and it's crossing from fashion into beauty. **Chartreuse** is the other colour that only has TikTok views, watched rather than searched.

### Veiled Performance: gorpcore grows up and brings an umbrella

[CHART: 06-palette-veiled-performance.png, the four Veiled Performance colours, marigold, moss green, dove grey and terracotta]

The sporty, rain-ready palette, and the name is less poetic than it sounds. Gorpcore has matured past the sports-prep peak of 2026 into sleek utility, nylon, drawstrings, hoods, and unpredictable weather is pushing rain jackets and waterproof footwear into everyday wardrobes. On my Paris tagging it's still a one-house story, nylon and technical fabric were in 4% of all looks and 26% of Lacoste's, and PVC was Chloé alone, on six trench coats and capes. So the clothes are early, but the colours aren't.

The colours: **marigold** is the most searched colour in the entire forecast, 31% of the combined searches for the five colours that have a volume, and its neighbour amberglow is up 50% in Google searches over the last three months in my own pull. **Dove grey** is the fastest climber of the twelve, up 43% on last year, **moss green** is up 23%, and **terracotta** is up 7%. I'm sorry, but a colour whose searches grew 7% in a year is not a forecast, it's a colour that exists.

> **In The Press**
> Here's the part I didn't expect. WGSN and Coloro published their four key colours for Autumn/Winter 2027/28 back in September 2025, a full year before this forecast: Russet, Peaceful Lilac, Maize and Deep Green ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/wgsn-and-coloro-key-colours-for-autumn-winter-2027-28/2025091783967)). That's russet red and forest green from Heritage Craft, lilac from Sensory Riot, and a maize yellow sitting right next to Veiled Performance's marigold. Two forecasters with different methods, one colour from each mood plus a second from Heritage Craft. When two independent sources land on the same four colours, that's a stronger claim than either alone, and it's the shortlist I'd actually take seriously.

## Same scale: which of the twelve are actually being searched?

Ok, ok… twelve colours, three moods, each with a number next to it, very convincing. But a "high" search volume and an "extreme" one turn out to be two numbers barely a third apart, and only five of the twelve have a search volume at all. So here are the colours that share a measure, on one scale and in percentages.

### By share of searches: marigold first, the heritage colours last

[CHART: 11-colour-search-share.png, each colour's share of the combined searches for the five colours that come with a search volume]

Marigold takes 31% of the combined searches, lilac 25%, fuchsia 21%, and the two Heritage Craft colours sit at the bottom, forest green at 14% and dark red at 9%. Which means that of the four colours both forecasters agree on, the search data loves two (marigold and lilac) and shrugs at the other two (forest green and russet).

### By search growth: dove grey climbs, terracotta barely moves

[CHART: 09-colour-search-growth.png, % change in searches vs last year, for the five colours measured that way]

Dove grey is up 43% on last year, plum purple 39%, moss green 23%, cobalt blue 14% and terracotta 7%. Plum is the one I believe, because it's been building all year on this autumn's runways, which is where the press comes in. Chestnut brown and chartreuse have only TikTok views, so they sit outside both charts. Twelve colours, three different yardsticks, not one colour measured twice. That's not dishonest, it's how forecasts are built, but it's exactly why I don't call all twelve a trend.

> **In The Press**
> Plum and purple are already this autumn's story. Who What Wear has been calling plum the elegant cousin of burgundy for Winter 2026 ([Who What Wear](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/trends/plum-color-trend-winter-2026-womens-outfits)), and Coveteur had royal purple taking red's place as the colour of the Fall/Winter 2026 season, worn head to toe at Loewe, Ferragamo and Tory Burch ([Coveteur](https://coveteur.com/royal-purple-trend)). So plum purple's growth is riding a wave that started on the FW26 runways, which makes it the safest bet in the Heritage Craft palette even though its cousin russet has the weakest search number.

## What the Paris SS27 runway can already see

[CHART: paris/donut-season.png, the Paris SS27 season palette, % = share of all colour on the runway across 16 houses]

The forecast calls its runway references early indicators, so it's fair to check the twelve colours against the SS27 runway I tagged, with the caveat that spring and autumn palettes are different animals. Grouping the twelve into the families my data uses, brown and beige was 11% of the Paris palette (chestnut brown's family), green and olive 8% (forest and moss), grey 7% (dove grey), pink, blue and yellow 5% each (fuchsia, cobalt, marigold and chartreuse), orange and rust 3% (terracotta), red 2% (russet) and purple 1% (plum and lilac). So the only forecast colour Paris was already properly wearing is green, the biggest real colour of the season, and the Heritage Craft anchors, russet and plum, were close to absent, which doesn't make them wrong for autumn, it means the runway isn't where that signal is coming from yet. The fabric fairs are: Première Vision Paris built its Autumn/Winter 2027/28 season on deep purples, bordeaux and mineral neutrals ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fairs/premiere-vision-paris-what-fabrics-reveal-about-aw27-28-trends/2026090490139)), which is the Heritage Craft palette almost exactly.

### House by house, the Paris houses already wearing next autumn

This is my favourite bit, because the season average hides where the colour actually lives, and a few Paris houses were already in the FW27 palette without knowing it.

- **Lacoste** is the Veiled Performance house. Green is its biggest slice at 24% of its palette, mostly khaki and bottle green, orange and rust is 12% and literally tagged as terracotta, clay and rust, and yellow is 11%, mostly chartreuse. Three of the forecast's twelve colours in one collection, against a season average of 8%, 3% and 5%.
- **Loewe** owns marigold's family, yellow and gold at 15% of its palette (canary and lemon sorbet), three times the season average, and green at 20%, though Loewe's green is jade and pistachio, nothing like forest.
- **Miu Miu** is the chestnut brown house, brown and beige at 20% of its palette, tagged as chocolate, brown check and chestnut leather, against 11% for the season.
- **Valentino** did the Sensory Riot colours, pink at 13% of its palette and specifically fuchsia and cerise, plus lavender at 6% of its palette, which with Balmain's 8% is where almost all of Paris's purple came from.
- **Chanel, Maison Margiela and Dior** are the dove grey houses, 15%, 16% and 13% against 7% for the season.
- **Russet red** has no house. Red was 2% of Paris and Chanel and Lanvin gave it 7% each, scarlet and lacquer red, neither of them a russet. If the forecasters are right about russet, it's coming from nowhere on the spring runway.

## The Takeaway

What the FW27 colour forecast is actually made of:

- Three moods with real reasons behind them: quiet luxury growing up into craft, a maximalist reaction to AI sameness and minimalism fatigue, and gorpcore maturing into rain-ready utility.
- Four colours two forecasters agree on: russet red, lilac, a maize or marigold yellow, and forest green. That's the shortlist.
- Of those four, search backs two, marigold (31% of the combined searches) and lilac (25%), and shrugs at forest green (14%) and dark red (9%).
- Plum purple is the safest Heritage Craft bet, up 60% in my own Google pull and 39% in the forecast's, on the back of this autumn's purple runways, with dark red and forest green both up 50% behind it and chestnut brown at 10%.
- Paris SS27 already wore green (8% of the palette, Lacoste 24%, Loewe 20%), brown (11%, Miu Miu 20%) and grey (7%, Margiela 16%), and almost no red or purple, so the heritage reds and plums are a fabric fair story, not a runway one yet.
- Twelve colours, three yardsticks, none measured twice. Line them up and the ones worth your money are marigold, lilac and plum.

If I'm buying one colour for next autumn it's marigold, because two forecasters and the search data agree, and Loewe already did it. Which one are you betting on? (I need to know I'm not the only one who's going to look like a tiny sun.)

## Full methodology

The twelve colours and the three aesthetics are from the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast Fall/Winter 2027, published October 2026, which prints one data point next to each colour: an absolute search volume for five (marigold, lilac, fuchsia, forest green, dark red), a year on year change in searches for five (dove grey, plum purple, moss green, cobalt blue, terracotta) and TikTok views for two (chestnut brown, chartreuse). To make them readable I show the five volumes as each colour's share of their combined total (the donut), the five growth figures as printed, and leave the two view counts out of the charts, because views and searches don't compare. The forecast doesn't define the period or country behind the search volumes, and the piece says so. Hex codes on the palette cards are sampled from the forecast's own swatches.

The extra search figures in the palette sections (cotton up 90% and cashmere up 80% this year against last, jacquard jackets up 61% on last year, peplum jackets up 24%, blush pink up 20%, amberglow up 50% and blue up 33% over the last three months, harness boots up 476% this year, plum purple up 60% on Google and 154% on TikTok, dark red up 50%, forest green up 50%, chestnut brown up 10%) are my own Google Trends and TikTok pulls, worldwide, periods as stated, and they sit alongside the forecast's figures rather than replacing them, which is why plum purple carries two growth numbers.

The WGSN and Coloro Autumn/Winter 2027/28 key colours (Russet, Peaceful Lilac, Maize, Deep Green) are from their September 2025 announcement as reported by FashionUnited, and the match to the forecast's colours is my reading of the names and swatches, not a measured colour distance.

The runway cross-check uses my own tagging of 16 houses from Paris Fashion Week Spring Summer 2027, 997 looks, every show complete: Balenciaga, Balmain, Celine, Chanel, Chloé, Dior, Dries Van Noten, Isabel Marant, Lacoste, Lanvin, Loewe, Maison Margiela, Miu Miu, Stella McCartney, Tom Ford and Valentino. Every look is split across its main colours, up to three, by how much of the outfit each covers, so each palette adds up to 100% and all shades of one family sit in one slice. Material and embellishment figures are the share of all 997 looks that include them. To compare with the forecast I grouped its twelve colours into those families (russet red into red, plum purple and lilac into purple, forest and moss green into green and olive, chestnut brown into brown and beige, chartreuse and marigold into yellow and gold, fuchsia into pink, cobalt into blue, dove grey into grey, terracotta into orange and rust), which is a family match, not a shade match. SS27 is a different season from the one forecast, so the cross-check shows what the spring runway can already see, not whether the forecast is right.
