# Stop Calling Everything a Trend: Fall/Winter 2027 Colour Edition

## Two forecasters land on the same four colours for next autumn, the search data backs two of them, and the Paris runway mostly backs one

I said in the [FW27 trends piece](LINK TO FW27 TRENDS PIECE) that colour would get its own post, and here it is, because the colour story in that forecast turned out to be better than the trends one. Twelve colours for next autumn, split into three moods, each with one number next to it, and when I lined them up something I didn't expect happened: a second forecaster, working from a completely different method, had picked four of the same colours a year earlier. So this piece is about which of the twelve the forecasters agree on, which ones people are actually searching for, and which ones the Paris SS27 runway, the one I tagged myself, can already see.

## Methodology, the short version

The Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast for Fall/Winter 2027 names twelve colours, four for each of its three aesthetics, Heritage Craft, Sensory Riot and Veiled Performance, and gives each colour exactly one number, a search volume, a year on year change in searches, or TikTok views. I ranked the colours within each measure, exactly as printed, then checked them against two outside sources: the WGSN and Coloro key colours for the same season, and my own Paris SS27 colour tagging, 16 houses and 997 looks, where every look is split across up to three colours by how much of the outfit each covers. Full detail at the bottom.

## The three palettes

### Heritage Craft: russet red, plum purple, forest green, chestnut brown

[CHART: 06-palette-heritage-craft.png, the four Heritage Craft colours, hex sampled from the forecast's own swatches, with the one data point each comes with]

The stealth wealth palette, autumn leaves and riding boots. Dark red comes with a search volume of 35k, forest green with 57k, plum purple with searches up 39% on last year, and chestnut brown with 196M TikTok views.

### Sensory Riot: chartreuse, fuchsia, lilac, cobalt blue

[CHART: 06-palette-sensory-riot.png, the four Sensory Riot colours, hex sampled from the forecast's own swatches, with the one data point each comes with]

The maximalist palette, and the one with the biggest numbers after marigold: fuchsia at 84k searches, lilac at 103k, chartreuse at 149M TikTok views, and cobalt blue up 14% on last year.

### Veiled Performance: marigold, moss green, dove grey, terracotta

[CHART: 06-palette-veiled-performance.png, the four Veiled Performance colours, hex sampled from the forecast's own swatches, with the one data point each comes with]

The sporty, rain-ready palette. Marigold has the biggest search number of the whole forecast at 126k, dove grey is up 43% on last year, moss green up 23% and terracotta up 7%.

> **In The Press**
> Here's the part I didn't expect. WGSN and Coloro published their four key colours for Autumn/Winter 2027/28 back in September 2025, a full year before this forecast: Russet, Peaceful Lilac, Maize and Deep Green ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/wgsn-and-coloro-key-colours-for-autumn-winter-2027-28/2025091783967)). That's russet red and forest green from Heritage Craft, lilac from Sensory Riot, and a maize yellow sitting right next to Veiled Performance's marigold. Two forecasters with different methods, one colour from each of the three moods plus a second from Heritage Craft. When two independent sources land on the same four colours, that's a stronger claim than either alone, and it's the shortlist I'd actually take seriously.

## Same scale, head to head: which of the twelve are actually being searched?

Ok, ok… twelve colours, each with a number, very convincing. But a "high" search volume and an "extreme" one turn out to be 35k and 57k, and only five of the twelve have a search volume at all. So here's every colour that shares a measure, on one scale.

### By search volume: marigold first, the heritage colours last

[CHART: 08-colour-search-volume.png, search volume as printed in the forecast, in thousands of searches, for the five colours measured that way]

Marigold is the most searched colour in the forecast at 126k, then lilac at 103k and fuchsia at 84k, and the two Heritage Craft colours sit at the bottom, forest green at 57k and dark red at 35k. Which means that of the four colours both forecasters agree on, the search data loves two (marigold and lilac) and shrugs at the other two (forest green and russet). "Dark red has a high search volume" is doing a lot of work for the smallest number on the chart. The forecast doesn't say which period or country these volumes cover, so I'm quoting them as given.

### By search growth: dove grey is the climber, terracotta barely moved

[CHART: 09-colour-search-growth.png, % change in searches vs last year, as printed in the forecast, for the five colours measured that way]

Dove grey is up 43% on last year, plum purple 39%, moss green 23%, cobalt blue 14% and terracotta 7%. I'm sorry, but a colour whose searches grew 7% in a year is not a forecast, it's a colour that exists. Plum is the one I believe, because it's been building all year on this autumn's runways, which is where the press comes in below.

### And the two that only have TikTok views

Chestnut brown has 196M views and chartreuse 149M, which tells you people are watching them, not that they're searching for them, and views don't compare with either chart above. Twelve colours, three different yardsticks, not one colour measured twice. That's not dishonest, it's how forecasts are built, but it's exactly why I don't call all twelve a trend.

> **In The Press**
> Plum and purple are already this autumn's story. Who What Wear has been calling plum the elegant cousin of burgundy for Winter 2026 ([Who What Wear](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/trends/plum-color-trend-winter-2026-womens-outfits)), and Coveteur had royal purple taking red's place as the colour of the Fall/Winter 2026 season, worn head to toe at Loewe, Ferragamo and Tory Burch ([Coveteur](https://coveteur.com/royal-purple-trend)). So plum purple's 39% search growth is riding a wave that started on the FW26 runways, which makes it the safest bet in the Heritage Craft palette even though its cousin russet has the weakest search number.

## What the Paris SS27 runway can already see

[CHART: 10-paris-families.png, % of the Paris SS27 colour palette already in each forecast colour family, from the DFB tagging of 16 houses and 997 looks]

The forecast calls its runway references early indicators, so it's fair to check the twelve colours against the SS27 runway I tagged, with the caveat that spring and autumn palettes are different animals. Grouping the twelve into the families my data uses, brown and beige was 11% of the Paris palette (chestnut brown's family), green and olive 8% (forest and moss), grey 7% (dove grey), pink, blue and yellow 5% each (fuchsia, cobalt, marigold and chartreuse), orange and rust 3% (terracotta), red 2% (russet) and purple 1% (plum and lilac). So the only forecast colour Paris was already properly wearing is green, the biggest real colour of the season, and the Heritage Craft anchors, russet and plum, were close to absent, which doesn't make them wrong for autumn, it means the runway isn't where that signal is coming from yet. The fabric fairs are: Première Vision Paris built its Autumn/Winter 2027/28 season on deep purples, bordeaux and mineral neutrals ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fairs/premiere-vision-paris-what-fabrics-reveal-about-aw27-28-trends/2026090490139)), which is the Heritage Craft palette almost exactly.

[CHART: paris/donut-season.png, the Paris SS27 season palette, % = share of all colour on the runway across 16 houses]

### House by house, the Paris houses already wearing next autumn

This is my favourite bit, because the season average hides where the colour actually lives, and a few Paris houses were already in the FW27 palette without knowing it.

- **Lacoste** is the Veiled Performance house. Green is its biggest slice at 24% of its palette, mostly khaki and bottle green, orange and rust is 12% and literally tagged as terracotta, clay and rust, and yellow is 11%, mostly chartreuse. Three of the forecast's twelve colours in one collection, against a season average of 8%, 3% and 5%.
- **Loewe** owns marigold's family, yellow and gold at 15% of its palette (canary and lemon sorbet), three times the season average, and green at 20%, though Loewe's green is jade and pistachio, nothing like forest.
- **Miu Miu** is the chestnut brown house, brown and beige at 20% of its palette, tagged as chocolate, brown check and chestnut leather, against 11% for the season.
- **Valentino** did the Sensory Riot colours, pink at 13% of its palette and specifically fuchsia and cerise, plus lavender at 6% of its palette, which with Balmain's 8% is where almost all of Paris's purple came from.
- **Chanel, Maison Margiela and Dior** are the dove grey houses, 15%, 16% and 13% against 7% for the season.
- **Russet red** has no house. Red was 2% of Paris and Chanel and Lanvin gave it 7% each, scarlet and lacquer red, neither of them a russet. If the forecasters are right about russet, it's coming from nowhere on the spring runway.

[CHART: paris/grid-houses-fw27.png, Lacoste, Loewe, Miu Miu and Valentino SS27 palettes, % = share of each house's total colour on the runway]

## The Takeaway

What the FW27 colour forecast is actually made of:

- Four colours two forecasters agree on: russet red, lilac, a maize or marigold yellow, and forest green. That's the shortlist.
- Of those four, search backs two, marigold at 126k and lilac at 103k, and shrugs at forest green (57k) and dark red (35k).
- Plum purple is the safest Heritage Craft bet, up 39% in searches on the back of this autumn's purple runways, even though its anchor cousin russet has the weakest number on the page.
- Paris SS27 already wore green (8% of the palette, Lacoste 24%, Loewe 20%), brown (11%, Miu Miu 20%) and grey (7%, Margiela 16%), and almost no red or purple, so the heritage reds and plums are a fabric fair story, not a runway one yet.
- Twelve colours, three yardsticks, none measured twice. Line them up and the ones worth your money are marigold, lilac and plum.

If I'm buying one colour for next autumn it's marigold, because two forecasters and the search data agree, and Loewe already did it. Which one are you betting on? (I need to know I'm not the only one who's going to look like a tiny sun.)

## Full methodology

The source is the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast Fall/Winter 2027, published October 2026, which names twelve colours, four for each of three aesthetics, and prints one data point next to each: an absolute search volume for five (marigold, lilac, fuchsia, forest green, dark red), a year on year change in searches for five (dove grey, plum purple, moss green, cobalt blue, terracotta) and TikTok views for two (chestnut brown, chartreuse). The charts rank every colour within its own measure, exactly as printed, nothing rescaled. The forecast doesn't define the period or country behind the search volumes, and the piece says so. Hex codes on the palette cards are sampled from the forecast's own swatches. No search data of my own sits behind this piece.

The WGSN and Coloro Autumn/Winter 2027/28 key colours (Russet, Peaceful Lilac, Maize, Deep Green) are from their September 2025 announcement as reported by FashionUnited, and the match to the forecast's colours is my reading of the names and swatches, not a measured colour distance.

The runway cross-check uses my own tagging of 16 houses from Paris Fashion Week Spring Summer 2027, 997 looks, every show complete: Balenciaga, Balmain, Celine, Chanel, Chloé, Dior, Dries Van Noten, Isabel Marant, Lacoste, Lanvin, Loewe, Maison Margiela, Miu Miu, Stella McCartney, Tom Ford and Valentino. Every look is split across its main colours, up to three, by how much of the outfit each covers, so each palette adds up to 100% and all shades of one family sit in one slice. To compare with the forecast I grouped its twelve colours into those families (russet red into red, plum purple and lilac into purple, forest and moss green into green and olive, chestnut brown into brown and beige, chartreuse and marigold into yellow and gold, fuchsia into pink, cobalt into blue, dove grey into grey, terracotta into orange and rust), which is a family match, not a shade match. The named shades (bottle green, chartreuse, terracotta, canary, chocolate, fuchsia, lavender, scarlet) come from the shade detail of the same tagging. SS27 is a different season from the one forecast, so the cross-check shows what the spring runway can already see, not whether the forecast is right.
