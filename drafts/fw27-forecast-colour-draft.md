# Stop Calling Everything a Trend: The FW27 Colour and Trend Index

## The twelve colours and the key pieces forecasted for next fall/winter, split into three aesthetics, checked against the Paris SS27 runway, whether you work in fashion or just love it

Shoppers have changed what they want from their clothes. The pieces that sell now have to feel like something, last longer than a season and work on a Tuesday as well as a Saturday, and that is exactly why anyone planning an assortment for next autumn wants hard market data rather than a mood board. The Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast for Fall/Winter 2027 is built for that job: it takes what people are searching for, what they engage with on social media and what retailers are actually putting on the shelf, and turns all of it into three macro themes, Heritage Craft, Sensory Riot and Veiled Performance, each with its own colours, key items and fabrics. I've spent the last few weeks taking it apart.

The ULTIMATE forecast FW 2027 is here!! I scraped the internet, social media, the press, and crunched the numbers to give you the twelve colours for next autumn, split into three moods. So this piece is about why those three moods exist at all, which of the twelve the forecasters agree on, which ones people are actually searching for, and which ones the [Paris SS27 runway](https://thedatafashionbrief.substack.com/p/stop-calling-everything-a-trend-the-5fd), the one I tagged myself, can already see.

## Methodology, the short version

The twelve colours come from the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast for Fall/Winter 2027, four for each of its three aesthetics, Heritage Craft, Sensory Riot and Veiled Performance. Trendalytics pulls demand data from across the internet, searches, TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest and what retailers have on their shelves, combines it per colour, then reads the pattern: how big searches are now, how fast they're growing or declining, how that compares with last year, cross-checked against what the press, blogs and Reddit are saying. What lands in the report is one number per colour, in three different kinds, so I've turned them into percentages you can compare, and added my own search pull for all twelve so they sit on one scale. Then I checked them against the WGSN and Coloro key colours for the same season and my own Paris SS27 tagging, 16 houses and 997 looks. Full detail at the bottom.

### Heritage Craft: quiet luxury grows up, and for winter it comes in deep colour

Stealth wealth is evolving into heritage craft. Shoppers are moving from logos to natural fabrics, with cotton up 90% and cashmere up 80% in searches this year against last, because what people want is a timeless wardrobe of tried and true luxury, clothes that work as an enduring staple rather than a season's statement. My Paris SS27 tagging sees the foundation of it already: wool suiting was in 25% of all looks, the biggest material of the season. The craft side shows in jacquard jackets (up 61% in searches on last year), lace (4% of all Paris looks), shrunken blazers and peplum jackets (up 24% in searches over the last three months) and the equestrian tall boot look, with harness boots up 476% this year. Autumn leaves and riding boots, basically, and the palette is exactly that.

[CHART: 15-heritage-craft-searches.png, the fabrics and pieces behind Heritage Craft, % change in searches this year vs last, harness boots, cotton, cashmere and jacquard jacket]

The colours, in searches this year against last: **plum purple** is the one moving, up 60%, which makes it the safest bet of the four, with **dark red** and **forest green** both up 50% behind it and **chestnut brown** barely moving at 10%. The context backs the order. Plum is also up 154% on TikTok, while forest green, at 5% of the combined searches, and dark red, at 3%, are near the bottom of the twelve on volume, and plum itself is mid-table at 9%. Chestnut brown is the odd one out: 196M views on TikTok and a 10% rise in searches, which is the difference between a colour people watch and a colour people go looking for.

### Sensory Riot: dressing in things you can actually feel

This is the maximalist palette, and the reasoning behind it is three things at once. Shoppers tired of generic AI imagery are turning to tactile, layered, clashing-print dressing that a machine can't replicate, years of quiet luxury have dissolved into corporate sameness, and effort is back, clothes that visibly took time to make. My data agrees on the direction: 31% of the Paris SS27 looks I tagged carried embellishment (ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading, embroidery, feathers or appliqué), at every one of the 16 houses, and the fringe skirt is the clearest signal, up 71% in searches on last year and the biggest mover among the items the forecast measured that way. Minimalism isn't over though, 56% of the Paris colour weight was still neutral, so the loud side grows while the quiet side stays.

The colours, in searches this year against last: **cobalt blue** is the climber at 30%, and it's crossing from fashion into beauty, then **fuchsia** at 20%, with **lilac** and **chartreuse** both up just 10%. The context changes the picture a little. Lilac takes 9% of the combined searches and fuchsia 6%, so lilac is a colour people already search for a fair amount that isn't growing much, while fuchsia is smaller and still moving, which is no surprise to anyone here, because I already told you [pink is back](https://thedatafashionbrief.substack.com/p/is-pink-actually-back-or-just-another), pink was in 8% of Paris looks and present at 81% of the shows I analysed, and blush pink searches are up 20% over the last three months. Blue in general is up 33% over the last three months. Chartreuse is the surprise of the palette: 149M views on TikTok and only a 10% rise in searches, but 11% of the combined searches, which puts it third of the twelve, level with terracotta and behind only grey and marigold. People already look for it, they just aren't looking for it more than last year.

### Veiled Performance: gorpcore grows up and brings an umbrella

The sporty, rain-ready palette, and the name is less poetic than it sounds. Gorpcore has matured past the sports-prep peak of 2026 into sleek utility, nylon, drawstrings, hoods, and unpredictable weather is pushing rain jackets and waterproof footwear into everyday wardrobes. On my Paris tagging it's still a one-house story, nylon and technical fabric were in 4% of all looks and 26% of Lacoste's, and PVC was Chloé alone, on six trench coats and capes. So the clothes are early, but the colours aren't.

The colours, in searches this year against last: **moss green** is the surprise, up 60%, level with plum purple as the fastest climber of all twelve, then **marigold** and **dove grey** both up 30%, and **terracotta** up 10%. The context: marigold is also the second most searched colour of the twelve, 16% of the combined searches, beaten only by dove grey at 19%, and grey is grey, and its neighbour amberglow is up 50% over the last three months, so it has volume and growth at once. Terracotta is the one to watch here though, because it's barely growing but already takes 11% of the combined searches, level with chartreuse, so it's big and flat rather than small, and moss green, the fastest climber, is the least searched of the twelve at 3%, so it's climbing from almost nothing. I'm sorry, but a colour whose searches grew 10% in a year is not a forecast, it's a colour that exists.

> **In The Press**
> Here's the part I didn't expect. WGSN and Coloro published their four key colours for Autumn/Winter 2027/28 back in September 2025, a full year before this forecast: Russet, Peaceful Lilac, Maize and Deep Green ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/wgsn-and-coloro-key-colours-for-autumn-winter-2027-28/2025091783967)). That's russet red and forest green from Heritage Craft, lilac from Sensory Riot, and a maize yellow sitting right next to Veiled Performance's marigold. Two forecasters with different methods, one colour from each mood plus a second from Heritage Craft. When two independent sources land on the same four colours, that's a stronger claim than either alone, and it's the shortlist I'd actually take seriously.

## Same scale: all twelve colours, one measure

Ok, ok… twelve colours, three moods, each with a number next to it, very convincing. But the forecast measures them three different ways, a "high" search volume and an "extreme" one turn out to be two numbers barely a third apart, and only five of the twelve have a search volume at all. So I pulled all twelve myself, searches this year against last, and put them on one chart.

## What the Paris SS27 runway can already see

[CHART: paris/donut-season.png, the Paris SS27 season palette, % = share of all colour on the runway across 16 houses]

Spring and autumn palettes are different (we know that), but the forecast calls its runway references early indicators, so here is the check. Grouped into my colour families, Paris SS27 was 11% brown and beige (chestnut brown), 8% green and olive (forest and moss), 7% grey (dove grey), 5% each for pink, blue and yellow (fuchsia, cobalt, marigold and chartreuse), 3% orange and rust (terracotta), 2% red (russet) and 1% purple (plum and lilac). So green is the only forecast colour Paris was already properly wearing, and the Heritage Craft anchors, russet and plum, were close to absent, which doesn't make them wrong for autumn, it means the signal is coming from the fabric fairs, where Première Vision built its Autumn/Winter 2027/28 season on deep purples, bordeaux and mineral neutrals ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fairs/premiere-vision-paris-what-fabrics-reveal-about-aw27-28-trends/2026090490139)), not from the runway yet.

[CHART: 17-bridge-ss27-fw27.png, the spring runway vs what people search now, % of all colour on the Paris SS27 runway next to % of combined searches for the twelve forecast colours, by colour family]

Put the two side by side and the gap is the story. Yellow and gold were 5% of the Paris colour and are 27% of the searches, purple was 1% of the runway and is 18% of the searches, grey 7% against 19%. The other way round, brown and beige were 11% of what Paris wore and take 4% of the searches, so the runway is ahead on brown and the public is ahead on yellow, grey and purple, and green is the one family where the two agree, 8% and 8%.

A few houses were already in next autumn's palette without knowing it. **Lacoste** is the Veiled Performance house, green 24% of its palette (khaki and bottle green), orange and rust 12% (tagged terracotta, clay and rust) and yellow 11% (mostly chartreuse), against season averages of 8%, 3% and 5%. **Loewe** owns marigold's family, yellow and gold at 15%, three times the season average. **Miu Miu** is the chestnut brown house at 20% brown and beige. **Valentino** did the Sensory Riot colours, fuchsia and cerise at 13% and lavender at 6%. **Chanel, Maison Margiela and Dior** are the dove grey houses at 15%, 16% and 13% against 7%. And russet red has no house at all: red was 2% of Paris, scarlet at Chanel and lacquer red at Lanvin, neither of them a russet.

## The Takeaway

- Fastest growing: plum purple and moss green, both up 60% in searches this year against last.
- Most searched: dove grey at 19% of the combined searches, then marigold at 16%.
- Big but flat: chartreuse and terracotta, 11% each and up just 10%. Lilac and chestnut brown barely move.
- Paris SS27 already wore green, brown and grey, almost no red or purple.

**Safe to buy**: marigold (volume, growth and Loewe already did it), plum purple, tailored suiting (25% of Paris looks, up 133% in market adoption), anything fringed or embellished (fringe skirt up 71%, embellishment in 31% of Paris looks), jacquard jackets and paisley scarves (up 61% and 64%).

**Worth a bet**: moss green, dark red and forest green, growing fast from small volumes, and harness boots, up 476%.

**Not yet**: the rain gear (nylon was 4% of Paris looks, nearly all Lacoste), chestnut brown (watched on TikTok, not searched), and lilac, chartreuse and terracotta as anything new.

If I'm buying one colour for next autumn it's marigold, and if I'm buying two, the second is plum. Which one are you betting on?

## Full methodology

How Trendalytics gets to its numbers: the platform aggregates consumer demand signals from searches, social platforms (TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest) and retail assortment data, then tracks each colour or item on volume today, speed of growth or decline, and the change against the same period last year, with press, blogs and forum conversation used as a second, qualitative layer. Three of its measures appear in this piece: search volume (printed as an absolute figure with no period or country stated), change in searches on last year, and TikTok views. In the companion trends piece there are three more: market adoption, which Trendalytics uses for how many retailers carry an item compared with last year, growth among key influencers, and a luxury-saturation label. I'm describing the method as Trendalytics presents it, the report itself prints only the final figures.

The twelve colours and the three aesthetics are from the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast Fall/Winter 2027, published October 2026, which prints one data point next to each colour: an absolute search volume for five (marigold, lilac, fuchsia, forest green, dark red), a year on year change in searches for five (dove grey, plum purple, moss green, cobalt blue, terracotta) and TikTok views for two (chestnut brown, chartreuse). To make them readable I show the five growth figures as printed and keep the two view counts as context in the prose only, because views and searches don't compare on a chart. The share of searches chart uses a different set of volumes: search volume over the last three months for all twelve colours, exported from the Trendalytics platform, which covers more colours on one period than the five volumes printed in the report. Four shade names return nothing as search terms, so the nearest term people actually type stands in for them: dark grey for dove grey, deep purple for plum purple, dark brown for chestnut brown and dark green for moss green, and the chart says so. The same export's year on year column is not used, the growth figures in the piece are my own search pulls. The forecast doesn't define the period or country behind the search volumes, and the piece says so. Hex codes on the palette cards are sampled from the forecast's own swatches.

The extra search figures in the palette sections (cotton up 90% and cashmere up 80% this year against last, jacquard jackets up 61% on last year, peplum jackets up 24%, blush pink up 20%, amberglow up 50% and blue up 33% over the last three months, harness boots up 476% this year, and all twelve colours) are my own search and TikTok pulls, worldwide; the twelve colour figures are searches this year against last (plum purple 60, moss green 60, dark red 50, forest green 50, cobalt blue 30, marigold 30, dove grey 30, fuchsia 20, lilac 10, chartreuse 10, chestnut brown 10, terracotta 10), the other periods are as stated in the text, and they sit alongside the forecast's figures rather than replacing them, which is why plum purple carries two growth numbers.

The WGSN and Coloro Autumn/Winter 2027/28 key colours (Russet, Peaceful Lilac, Maize, Deep Green) are from their September 2025 announcement as reported by FashionUnited, and the match to the forecast's colours is my reading of the names and swatches, not a measured colour distance.

The runway cross-check uses my own tagging of 16 houses from Paris Fashion Week Spring Summer 2027, 997 looks, every show complete: Balenciaga, Balmain, Celine, Chanel, Chloé, Dior, Dries Van Noten, Isabel Marant, Lacoste, Lanvin, Loewe, Maison Margiela, Miu Miu, Stella McCartney, Tom Ford and Valentino. Every look is split across its main colours, up to three, by how much of the outfit each covers, so each palette adds up to 100% and all shades of one family sit in one slice. Material and embellishment figures are the share of all 997 looks that include them. To compare with the forecast I grouped its twelve colours into those families (russet red into red, plum purple and lilac into purple, forest and moss green into green and olive, chestnut brown into brown and beige, chartreuse and marigold into yellow and gold, fuchsia into pink, cobalt into blue, dove grey into grey, terracotta into orange and rust), which is a family match, not a shade match. SS27 is a different season from the one forecast, so the cross-check shows what the spring runway can already see, not whether the forecast is right.
