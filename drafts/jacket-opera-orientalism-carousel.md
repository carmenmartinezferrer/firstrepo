## Slide 3, what the press is saying

Who What Wear calls evening jackets one of the most dominant trends of the Spring/Summer 2027 runways, and the reason it gives is that they are pieces you build an entire outfit around [S1]. Tapestry and jacquard jackets came through the same season at Ralph Lauren, Saint Laurent and Acne Studios, according to Hello! [S2].

Which means the jacket is no longer the last thing you add to an outfit, and is increasingly the first thing you choose, with everything else picked to suit it.

## Slide 4, the test (this one is for the reader)

there are two ways to wear a jacket, and one of them takes over.

**The jacket as a statement** sits on top of an outfit that already works, so if you take it off, you still have a look.

**The jacket as the outfit** is the whole idea, and if you take it off, what's left is a vest and some trousers you'd never have chosen on their own.

try it with your own wardrobe, and be honest about which one you bought.

## Slide 5, the opera coat

The piece driving this is the opera coat, a long, robe-like evening jacket in floral silk, brocade or velvet, often trimmed with fur or feathers at the cuffs [S3]. Alexa Chung, Zoë Kravitz and Kate Moss have all been photographed in vintage ones [S4], and Kendall Jenner has been spotted in a Conner Ives version [S5].

On the runway, Michael Kors staged his Fall/Winter 2026 anniversary show at the Metropolitan Opera with opera coats and long gloves [S6], and Conner Ives opened his Fall/Winter 2026 show with a full-length silk coat made from repurposed vintage fur [S6].

## Slide 6, what it's made of

The fabrics are where this gets interesting. Coverage describes the coats in chinoiserie silks and intricate brocades [S3], and the Conner Ives opening look was reported as using Qing Dynasty era tapestries [S6, verify before publishing].

Chinoiserie is the European habit of borrowing Chinese motifs as decoration, and it has been in Western wardrobes for centuries, which is why a trend that looks brand new can still carry a very old way of seeing.

## Slide 7, the orientalism question

Orientalism is the word for Western culture presenting "the East" as a single, decorative, faraway place, and the critics who use it about fashion are usually pointing at the blurring. One 2026 critique of Valentino's Resort 2026 collection says the references merge into one vague, distant "Orient" instead of naming specific cultures [S7], and another argues that Prada only described a South Asian inspired design as "inspired" after public backlash [S7].

Neither of those is a Chinese reference, so I'm not saying the opera coat is the same case. What the opera coat does share with them is the question of whether the source is named.

## Slide 8, how to tell homage from costume

Three things I'd check before buying into any "inspired by" piece, which is my suggestion for you to keep or cut:

1. Is the actual garment or craft named (a Qing tapestry, a Peking opera robe, a mandarin collar), or just "Oriental" or "exotic"?
2. Does the maker of the original get credited, or the people who still make it today?
3. Is it styled as a costume for one night, or woven into how the designer works season after season?
