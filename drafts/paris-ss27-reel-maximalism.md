**Full script (read-through):**
Maximalism got very loud this season, so loud everyone's saying it's killing quiet luxury. So I tagged every single look in Paris Fashion Week to check. And plot twist: 56% of Paris was still neutral. But colour snuck in everywhere. Pink showed up at 13 of the 16 houses. And maximalism is basically matching things that were never supposed to match. Print on print, the combination you look at and think "how is this working?". Dries and Miu Miu did it the most. Then texture: textured fabrics in almost a fifth of all looks, and metallics at 14 of the 16 houses. But here's the number. A third of every look in Paris had ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading or embroidery, at every single house. Dior did it in 70% of its looks, Chloé 69%, Isabel Marant 60%. Same neutral base, turned all the way up. So quiet luxury or maximalism? Paris said both: a quiet wardrobe wearing a lot of jewellery. Which one are you?

**Data check:**
- ✅ 56% of the Paris colour palette is neutral (black, white/cream, grey, brown/beige), Overview and Colour mix donut tabs
- ✅ Pink at 13 of 16 houses (a pink slice in 13 house palettes, 11 of them at 1% or more), Colour mix donut tab
- ✅ Print on print: 18 looks with two or more different prints, Dries Van Noten 5 and Miu Miu 5 lead, Garments tab. Kept as a house story, not a season %, because it's only 2% of looks
- ✅ Textured finish in 18% of looks (15 houses), metallic in 5% of looks at 14 houses, Trends tab
- ✅ 31% of looks have at least one of ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading, embroidery, 3D flowers, feathers or appliqué, at all 16 houses, Looks tab
- ✅ Embellished share of each house's looks: Dior 70%, Chloé 69%, Isabel Marant 60%, Looks tab
- ⚠️ "A quiet wardrobe wearing a lot of jewellery" is a metaphor for embellishment, not a jewellery stat (necklaces are in 8% of looks). Fine as a line, just don't put a jewellery number next to it
- ⚠️ If you want "killing quiet luxury" as a firm claim instead of a question, you need Google Trends data on "quiet luxury" and "maximalism"
