**Full script (read-through, about 125 words plus Ben):**
Just hear me out on this one, because the next designer bag you buy probably won't be new, and today I'm going to show you why. Remember when secondhand was where you went to pay less? Well, this year Fashionphile paid 15% over the boutique price for a used Chanel bag, the first one Matthieu Blazy designed. Meanwhile, searches for vintage on the platform are up 112%. So people are paying more for used, and looking for older. But why? [BEN] After years of price rises and campaigns criticised for looking AI-made, the customer doesn't want new, she wants something with a human behind it that nobody else has. So secondhand isn't the second shop any more, it's the first one. I'm a fashion data analyst, so if you want to understand fashion differently, follow me for more.

**Check before posting:**
- The 15%: WWD's wording is that Fashionphile paid sellers 15% above retail for the Urban Essential. Ask Ben whether it is what they paid the seller or what the bag sold for, and change the line to match.
- The 112%: taken from press coverage of the report, not read on the report page, so confirm it there or with the PR.
- "After years of price rises..." is Carmen's read, not a sourced finding. If Ben's answer says something different, that sentence follows him.
- "Campaigns criticised for looking AI-made" is reported criticism (Gucci, February 2026, Valentino, December 2025), keep the word "criticised".
- Ben's name and title on screen: as he asks to be introduced.
