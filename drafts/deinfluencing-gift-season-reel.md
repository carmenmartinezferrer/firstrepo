# Reel: Deinfluencing you with data, how to buy less this gift season

Format: talking head, same shape as the "romantic maximalism 2027" prediction Reel Carmen pasted (structure borrowed, wording and content hers). About 215 words, 65 to 75 seconds.

**Hook (on-screen, frame 1):** How to buy less this gift season, from a fashion data analyst

## Full script (read-through)

If you feel like every gift guide is telling you to buy more, I have really good news for this gift season, because my biggest tip as a fashion data analyst is to buy less, and I think it could be the smartest shopping shift you make this year.

Basically, everything comes down to what it's made of and how long you'll wear it.

For example, natural fibres are so big right now, searches for wool have doubled in a year, and cotton and cashmere aren't far behind, and so are deep colours like plum, dark red, forest green and cobalt blue, which I'd wear as the new neutrals.

Picture your grandma's wardrobe, but in your size.

I think we're moving towards shopping that feels slower, more considered and almost boring, in a good way.

If I had to pick one piece, it's the midi skirt, because searches are up 250% on last year and it's the least trendy thing on my list.

We're also seeing fur trim jackets, paisley scarves, chunky belts and round sunglasses climbing.

Basically, I think this season is about buying one good thing instead of five, about checking the label before the logo, and about pieces that do more than one job.

And maybe it's also the year we stop buying a new accessory for every outfit, because one silk scarf goes at the neck, in the hair, on the bag or around the waist as a cummerbund. You only need the one.
