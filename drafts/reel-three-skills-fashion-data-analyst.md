**Full script (read-through, 203 words, roughly 80 seconds at a relaxed pace):**

I use AI for the automated stuff, like coding, but the three skills that make me valuable as a fashion data analyst have nothing to do with it. AI makes information and output cheap and endless, so the people who matter are the ones who can make sense of the noise and make people care. Number one is asking the right questions, because the data will answer anything you ask it, but you have to find the key questions that solve the problem, and narrow them down until they are as concrete as possible. Number two is finding a proxy when the data is not there, which is basically knowing how to read the signs, since nobody hands you a number for what people want to wear, so you have to find the data that points to it and make it useful. If something is going up in popularity or there is a spike, that is demand, but will it last? That is the question that matters. And number three is storytelling, the most important one, very simple storytelling that hooks people. None of this needs a new tool, which is exactly why anyone can learn it, so which one is already yours?

## Why the three sit in this order (the peak curve)

Asking the right questions is the most recognisable, so it opens the climb and lets the viewer nod. Reading the signs is the surprise, and its closing question, whether a spike will last, is the line that makes people stay for skill 3. Storytelling goes last because it is your strongest opinion and the one the viewer is most likely to repeat, and the close then hands them something to answer in the comments rather than summarising.
