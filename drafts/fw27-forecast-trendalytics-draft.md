# Stop Calling Everything a Trend: Fall/Winter 2027 Forecast Edition

## Trendalytics gave me 36 trends for next autumn, and lining them up on one scale changes which ones look real

Forecast season is here, which means my inbox is full of PDFs telling me what I'll be wearing a year from now, and I have a complicated relationship with them. I love them (I read every single one, I'm not going to lie), and I also know exactly how they're built, because every item gets the one number that makes it look good. So when the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast for Fall/Winter 2027 landed, with its three aesthetics and 36 named colours, fabrics and key items, I did the thing I always do, which is ignore the moodboards for a second and ask which of those 36 are actually moving, which ones I can already see in my own runway data from this season, and which ones are there because the slide needed a fourth square. Also, I had already decided I'm not buying a plaid anything this year, and then I saw the riding boots on page two, so let's see how long that lasts.

## Methodology, the short version

Trendalytics splits next autumn into three aesthetics, Heritage Craft, Sensory Riot and Veiled Performance, and gives each one four colours, four fabrics or prints, three runway references and four key items, with one data point attached to each. The problem for anyone trying to compare them is that those data points come in six different flavours (search volume, year on year search growth, year on year market adoption, TikTok views, growth among key influencers, and a "most saturated in luxury" label with no number at all), and no item gets more than one. So the first thing I did was sort all 36 by the metric they share and plot each group on the same scale, head to head, which is the same step that found the real story in my NYFW and Paris pieces. Then I checked the items that overlap with my own Paris SS27 tagging, 16 houses and 997 looks, to see which of them the runway can already see. The full detail is at the bottom.

## What Trendalytics is forecasting, aesthetic by aesthetic

### Heritage Craft: stealth wealth gets a brooch

This is the one Trendalytics calls an evolution of stealth wealth with an artisanal touch, and the moodboard is exactly what you'd imagine, brooches, riding boots, a cashmere capelet and peplum silhouettes, in a palette of russet red, plum purple, forest green and chestnut brown. The fabrics are plaid, lace, painterly florals and tweed, the runway references are Chanel, Ralph Lauren and Acne Studios, and the key items are Mary Jane pumps, jacquard jackets, paisley scarves and slim tailored suiting. The macro story behind it has three legs: consumers moving away from logos towards natural fibres like cashmere and cotton, a European influence that Trendalytics ties to the EU's new ban on destroying unsold goods pushing designer stock into US off-price channels, and economic uncertainty sending people back to classics with a bookish, romantic twist.

A quick check on that middle leg, because it's a policy claim and not a vibe. The ban is real, large companies have been barred from destroying unsold clothing, accessories and footwear across the EU since 19 July 2026, with medium-sized companies following in 2030 ([Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/eu-ends-practice-of-destroying-unsold-clothes-and-shoes-in-july-2026/)). Whether that stock ends up on American off-price racks specifically is Trendalytics' reading of what happens next, not something the regulation says, so take that part as a forecast rather than a fact.

> **In The Press**
> The Chanel reference holds up. Matthieu Blazy's SS27 show was built on tweed, including a new stretch "smocked tweed", a windowpane tweed with mesh panels and a red and white tiger print version ([Reuters](https://whtc.com/2026/10/05/chanel-unveils-tiger-print-tweed-and-sheer-skirt-suits-in-spring-summer-paris-show/)), and [RUSSH](https://www.russh.com/chanel-spring-summer-2027/) had knee-length skirts of every variety reigning supreme. Ralph Lauren's New York show was framed around romance, and [Who What Wear](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/runway/ralph-lauren-new-york-fashion-week-spring-summer-2027) picked out a brocade jacket worn with ripped jeans, which is the jacquard jacket key item almost exactly. I couldn't find a review of Acne Studios SS27 that describes the painterly florals Trendalytics shows, so that reference stays unverified on my side.

### Sensory Riot: maximalism, again, and this time it's personal

Trendalytics' second aesthetic is maximalism with bold colour, mixed prints and tactile textures, and honestly it's the one I've been writing about all autumn, so I came to it with my arms crossed. The palette is chartreuse, fuchsia, lilac and cobalt blue, the fabrics are feather trim, animal stripes, lustrous silk and graphic prints, the runway references are Dries Van Noten, Dolce & Gabbana and Valentino, and the key items are a daytime boa, a fringe skirt, sculptural wedges and embellished jeans. The reasoning is three cultural pushes: algorithms rewarding high visual impact ("dopamine decor" and "printmaxxing"), a rebellion against AI-generated sameness in favour of things that look handmade and human, and plain minimalism fatigue after years of quiet luxury. Trendalytics also credits Chappell Roan for the mood, which I haven't checked and I'm not going to pretend I have.

The part I can check is the minimalism fatigue, because it's the argument I made in my [maximalism piece for FashionUnited](LINK TO FASHIONUNITED MAXIMALISM PIECE) with my own Paris SS27 data: 31% of all Paris looks carried some kind of embellishment (ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading, embroidery, 3D flowers, feathers or appliqué), and it reached every one of the 16 houses, with Dior at 70% of its looks, Chloé at 69% and Isabel Marant at 60%. Retail data points the same way, Lectra's summer 2026 assortment tracking had sequins up 40% and fringe up 34% year on year ([Lectra, via FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/press/fashion/fashion-trends-2026-between-minimalism-and-maximalism-lectra-presents-a-summer-of-contrasts-and-value/2026061888736)). So two independent sources agree with Trendalytics on the direction here, which is a stronger claim than any one of them alone. Where I'd push back is on "minimalism fatigue" meaning minimalism is leaving, because 56% of the Paris SS27 colour weight was still neutral, and Lanvin, Celine and Balenciaga showed collections that were three quarters neutral or more. The loud half is growing, and the quiet half hasn't gone anywhere.

> **In The Press**
> All three runway references check out. [WWD](https://wwd.com/runway/spring-2027/paris/dries-van-noten/review/) described Dries Van Noten's washed silks and satins throughout, and in my own Paris data Dries put silk in 72% of its looks, with graphic and floral print as its two biggest colour slices. Dolce & Gabbana's Milan show put leopard on outerwear, dresses, jackets and accessories across jacquard, satin, velvet and leather, plus tweed suits with leopard trim ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/d-g-reinvents-grand-sicilian-family-wardrobe/2026092890554)). And [AnOther](https://www.anothermag.com/fashion-beauty/17546/valentino-alessandro-michele-review-spring-summer-2027) had Alessandro Michele drawing on Valentino Garavani's 1980s work and describing the look as opulent and rich.

### Veiled Performance: gorpcore grows up and brings an umbrella

The third aesthetic is where sportswear and rugged utility meet sleek functionality, sheers, hoods, balloon pants and low profile sneakers, in marigold, moss green, dove grey and terracotta. The fabrics are nylon and PVC, acid wash denim, nature-inspired landscape graphics and camo, the runway references are Jane Wade, Chloé and Lacoste, and the key items are drawstring pants, a nylon bag, a funnel neck jacket and rain boots. The reasoning is gorpcore maturing after what Trendalytics calls the sports prep peak of 2026, weather getting more unpredictable and pushing rain jackets and waterproof footwear into everyday wardrobes, and a health and belonging angle where outdoor and sporty clothes signal community.

This is the aesthetic where I have the least of my own data, and I want to be upfront about it. Nylon and technical fabric were in 4% of all Paris SS27 looks, and 26% of Lacoste's own, so the Lacoste reference is real but it's mostly one house. PVC in Paris was exactly one house too, Chloé, which put it on six trench coats and capes while nobody else touched it. Drawstrings, funnel necks and hoods aren't tags in my taxonomy, so I genuinely can't tell you how big they were, and I'd rather say that than make a number up.

> **In The Press**
> Chloé's show is the strongest reference of the three. It rained during the show and Chemena Kamali had already planned for it, with [The Impression](https://theimpression.com/chloe-spring-2027-fashion-show-review/) describing a raincoat over lace, a fisherman's cape over chiffon and a black vinyl shell over a ruffled hem, plus a whole section of cape coats with flipped-up funnel necks, which is the funnel neck key item and the PVC fabric in one look. Jane Wade told [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2026/9/jane-wade-the-riverbed-spring-summer-2027-ss27) her SS27 collection swapped tool belt pockets for gathered nylon toggle drawcord pockets, the drawstring detail Trendalytics is pointing at. Lacoste's show at Roland-Garros mixed oversized polos, relaxed tailoring and technical pieces ([La Capital](https://www.lacapitalmdp.com/lacoste-transita-entre-la-estetica-urbana-y-deportiva-en-paris/)).

## Same scale, head to head: which of the 36 are actually moving?

Ok, ok… three aesthetics, 36 items, each with a number next to it, it all looks very convincing on the page. But a +7% and a +888% sit on the same slide in the same font size, and a "high" search volume and an "extreme" search volume turn out to be 35k and 57k. So here's every item that shares a metric, on one scale.

### Search volume: Veiled Performance owns the biggest number, and Heritage Craft the smallest

[CHART: 01-search-volume.png, search volume as reported by Trendalytics, in thousands of searches]

Six items come with an absolute search volume, and five of them are colours. Marigold, from the Veiled Performance palette, is the biggest search number in the entire report at 126k, ahead of lilac at 103k and fuchsia at 84k (both Sensory Riot), then lace at 78k, forest green at 57k and dark red at 35k. Which means the two colours Trendalytics builds the whole Heritage Craft palette around sit at the bottom of this list, and "dark red has a high search volume" is doing a lot of work for the smallest number on the chart. The report doesn't say which period or which country those volumes cover, so I'm reporting them as given rather than guessing.

### Search growth: fringe skirts lead, and two colours barely moved

[CHART: 02-search-growth.png, % change in searches vs last year, as reported by Trendalytics]

Eleven items come with a year on year change in searches, and this is where Sensory Riot and Heritage Craft take the top three between them: fringe skirts up 71%, paisley scarves up 64% and jacquard jackets up 61%. Dove grey (43%) and plum purple (39%) follow, then fur scarves (30%), moss green and wedges (23% each), acid wash denim (21%), and at the bottom cobalt blue at 14% and terracotta at 7%. I'm sorry, but a colour whose searches grew 7% in a year is not a forecast, it's a colour that exists. Fringe at the top matches what Heuritech saw in July, fringe detailing up 17% year on year with a further rise forecast for spring 2027 ([Heuritech](https://heuritech.com/articles/copenhagen-fashion-week-ss27-trends/)), and what Lectra saw in the summer assortments, so the fringe skirt is the one key item in this report with three independent sources behind it.

### Market adoption: florals and slim suits are the two real jumps

[CHART: 03-market-adoption.png, % change vs last year in Trendalytics' market adoption measure]

Ten items are measured by market adoption, Trendalytics' own measure of how widely the market has taken something on, and here Heritage Craft wins outright. Artistic florals are up 178% and slim suits up 133%, and nothing else is close: graphic prints 51%, camouflage 43%, nylon 38%, feather trim 36%, nylon bags 28%, landscape graphics 26%, embellished jeans 23% and plaid 21%. Plaid, which is the first fabric on the Heritage Craft page, is the smallest adoption move in the report. The slim suit number is the one I believe most, because Paris SS27 was a tailoring season in my data too, the straight trouser was in 13% of all looks and the single-breasted blazer in 11%, with Tom Ford, Maison Margiela, Celine and Miu Miu carrying the suiting. One honest caveat, "slim" isn't a fit I tag separately, so I can confirm the suit, not the slimness.

### The numbers that don't compare with anything

[CHART: 04-metric-mix.png, number of the 36 named trends measured each way]

Nine of the 36 items come with a metric shared by so few others that it can't be ranked. Chestnut brown has 196M TikTok views and chartreuse 149M, Mary Jane pumps 3M, and zebra print is "growing by 5M views weekly", which is a rate, not a total, so it doesn't even compare with the other three. Funnel neck jackets are up 888% among key influencers, rain boots 87% and drawstring pants 33%, and the report never says how many influencers or from what base, which matters, because 888% from almost nothing is exactly how a funnel neck starts to look like a movement. And tweed and silk are each "most saturated in the luxury market" with no number at all. This is my real finding in the report: 36 items, six different yardsticks, and not a single item measured two ways. That isn't dishonest, it's how forecasts are made, but it does mean the three aesthetics look equally strong on the page when the shared numbers say Heritage Craft and Sensory Riot are the ones moving, and Veiled Performance mostly isn't yet, apart from marigold.

## What my own Paris SS27 data can already see

[CHART: 05-paris-cross-check.png, % of all Paris SS27 looks that include each material]

Trendalytics frames its runway references as early indicators, so it's fair to check the forecast's fabrics against the SS27 runway, with the obvious caveat that SS27 is a different season from FW27 and a spring runway was never going to show much tweed. Of the materials that appear in both, silk is the one where the two sources flat out agree: Trendalytics has it as the most saturated fabric in luxury, and in Paris the silk family was in 28% of all looks across 15 of the 16 houses. Tweed is the interesting partial overlap, because "most saturated in luxury" sounds like everyone, but in Paris it was 5% of looks and almost all of it Chanel, which put tweed in 42% of its own collection, so saturated at one house and borrowed by a couple of neighbours. Lace was 4% of looks, concentrated at Isabel Marant, nylon 4% and mostly Lacoste, denim 4% (though I can't separate acid wash from the rest), animal print 3% with Balmain behind two thirds of it, and brocade and jacquard 2%. Plaid, feathers, camo and graphic prints don't have a clean material tag in my Paris data, so they stay unchecked.

## Colour, briefly

I'll keep this short because the full Paris colour breakdown lives in the [Paris SS27 colour index](LINK TO PARIS COLOUR PIECE). The three Trendalytics palettes are twelve colours, and only two of them are anywhere near neutral (dove grey and chestnut brown), which is a bold call against a Paris SS27 runway that was 56% neutral by colour weight. Of the forecast's colours, green was the biggest actual colour in Paris at 8% (Lacoste gave it 24% of its palette and Loewe 20%), pink was 5% but spread across 13 houses, red was 2% and purple 1%. So russet red and plum purple, the two anchors of Heritage Craft, were nearly absent on the spring runway, which doesn't make them wrong for autumn, it just means the runway isn't where that signal is coming from yet. The fabric fairs are more on Trendalytics' side, since Première Vision Paris built its Autumn/Winter 2027/28 season on deep purples, bordeaux and mineral neutrals ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fairs/premiere-vision-paris-what-fabrics-reveal-about-aw27-28-trends/2026090490139)), which is the Heritage Craft palette almost exactly. Anyway, I'll do a proper FW27 colour post once I have runway data for it.

## The matrix I couldn't build

Normally this is where I'd put the two-axis chart, trend alignment against colour intensity, and I'm not going to this time, for a reason that is itself worth knowing: not one of the 36 items in this report has two numbers attached to it, so there's no second axis to plot. The moment I have my own FW27 tagging and a search dataset side by side, it comes back.

## The Takeaway

What the FW27 forecast is actually made of:

- Heritage Craft has the strongest shared numbers in the report, artistic florals up 178% and slim suits up 133% in market adoption, and jacquard jackets and paisley scarves both up over 60% in searches, but its two anchor colours have the lowest search volumes on the page and were almost absent in Paris SS27.
- Sensory Riot has the most outside agreement: fringe skirts are the top search mover at 71% and the only item with three independent sources behind it, and my own Paris data has embellishment in 31% of looks at every house.
- Veiled Performance has the single biggest search number (marigold, 126k) and the weakest everything else, with most of its key items measured by influencer growth from an unstated base. The Chloé and Jane Wade references are real, but in Paris nylon and PVC were basically Lacoste and Chloé.
- Silk is the fabric where Trendalytics and my own runway data agree outright, and tweed is a Chanel story wearing a season-wide label.
- Thirty-six trends, six yardsticks, zero items measured twice. Line them up on one scale and the ones clearly moving are concentrated in two of the three aesthetics.

If I had to bet on one thing from this report actually reaching the shops, it's the fringe skirt, because search, retail and the runway all say the same thing. Which one are you already eyeing up? (I'm asking because I need to know I'm not the only one looking at riding boots.)

I hope you enjoyed it, and let me know in the comments anything you would like to know, doubts, if there's anything else you would like to know! I read you.

## Full methodology

The source is the Trendalytics Womenswear Forecast Fall/Winter 2027, published in October 2026, a 14 page PDF. It names three aesthetics (Heritage Craft, Sensory Riot and Veiled Performance), and for each one four colours, four fabrics or prints, three runway references from the SS27 shows and four key items, 36 named items in total, each with exactly one data point printed next to it. Those data points use six different measures: an absolute search volume (six items, five of them colours), a year on year change in searches (eleven items), a year on year change in Trendalytics' market adoption measure (ten items), TikTok views (four items, one of them a weekly rate), growth among key influencers (three items) and a "most saturated in the luxury market" label with no number (two items). The head to head charts rank every item within its own measure, exactly as printed, nothing is rescaled or estimated. The report doesn't define the period or the country behind the search volumes, how market adoption is calculated, or the size of the influencer panel, and the piece says so wherever it matters rather than filling the gaps in.

The runway cross-check uses my own tagging of 16 houses from Paris Fashion Week Spring Summer 2027, 997 looks, every show complete: Balenciaga, Balmain, Celine, Chanel, Chloé, Dior, Dries Van Noten, Isabel Marant, Lacoste, Lanvin, Loewe, Maison Margiela, Miu Miu, Stella McCartney, Tom Ford and Valentino. Every garment in every look was tagged by type, length, fit, material, finish, colour, pattern and detail. Material shares are the share of all 997 looks that include that material, so they overlap and don't add up to 100, and where I talk about a single house I mean its share of that house's own collection. The embellishment figure counts any look with ruffles, fringe, sequins, beading, embroidery, three-dimensional flowers, feathers or appliqué. Colour uses the colour mix method, where each look is split across up to three colours by how much of the outfit each one covers, and neutral means black, white and cream, grey, and brown and beige. SS27 is a different season from the one being forecast, so the cross-check tells you what the spring runway can already see, not whether the forecast is right.

No search validation of my own has been run for this piece, there are no Google Trends exports behind it, every search number here is Trendalytics' own. No alignment matrix has been built, because no item in the source carries two measures, so there's nothing to plot on a second axis.
