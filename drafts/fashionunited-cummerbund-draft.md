The cummerbund, the pleated sash traditionally worn at the waist of a tuxedo, has become the accessory of the party season, having moved from menswear formalwear on to the runways and into street style, where it is worn over shirts, knitwear, dresses and jeans. Demand for cummerbunds on Lyst rose 65 percent between August and September, according to data the shopping platform shared at the end of October, and on the runway it appeared in 38 percent of the Paris Spring/Summer 2027 collections, based on The Data Fashion Brief's analysis.

What makes it relevant for the holiday period is how little it asks of the customer. A cummerbund turns a shirt and trousers or a plain dress into an evening look without replacing any of them, and because it is in essence a band of fabric wrapped around the waist, many shoppers already own something that does the same job.

## Paris brings the waist back

The cummerbund's reach in Paris is part of a wider return to the defined waist. According to The Data Fashion Brief's analysis of Paris Fashion Week SS27, belted waists and corset-style tops appeared in 88 percent of the collections and almost half of all looks included a fitted piece, after several seasons in which soft and oversized shapes had led. The cummerbund is the accessory version of that shift, and it also suits the season's emphasis on layering, since it sits over a shirt, a knit or a blazer without changing what is underneath.

Valentino made the strongest case. Almost half of the looks in Alessandro Michele's show featured a version of the accessory, from suede and lace cummerbunds over satin bustiers to satin sashes over pleated trousers. Balenciaga took the opposite approach under Pierpaolo Piccioli, with ultra-wide satin sashes in contrasting teal and red worn low on the hips with oversized blazers, which exaggerated the proportions of the outfit rather than cinching it. That range is why The Data Fashion Brief reads the cummerbund as a styling device more than a single product, and as an easy way to add one block of colour to a plain outfit.

## An update that does not need a new purchase

The practical argument for the party season is that a cummerbund can be made from almost anything already in a wardrobe. It follows directly from the silk scarf belt of 2025, when printed scarves were folded in half and wrapped around the waist over jeans, skirts and dresses, and a folded scarf, a length of satin, a tie or a cummerbund from an existing formalwear set produces the same line at the waist as a new one. Marco Rambaldi made the point on the Spring/Summer 2027 runway, where printed fabric sashes were knotted at the waist over faded jeans and a metallic skirt.

The data has limits that are worth stating. The Lyst figure compares one month with the previous one, so it shows a sharp acceleration rather than a year of sustained demand, and no resale or rental data yet isolates the cummerbund, which means the case for reuse rests on how the piece is constructed and styled rather than on a measured figure.

## What this means for brands and retailers

For brands, the cummerbund is a low-risk way into the party season, an entry-price accessory in satin and silk that sells an evening look to a customer who is not buying a new dress. For retailers, it points towards styling it with existing stock, shirts, knitwear, tailored trousers and slip dresses, and towards treating scarves and sashes as part of the same waist story, since the customer can use them interchangeably.

Not every version is likely to last equally. The Data Fashion Brief's reading is that the theatrical interpretations, such as lace worn over a bustier, may have a short moment, while the plain satin sash over tailoring behaves like a belt, a category that does not leave the wardrobe. The first test is holiday trading over the coming weeks, and the second comes when the Autumn/Winter 2027 collections are shown in February and March, where another season of defined waists would move the cummerbund from a seasonal accessory to an established one.
