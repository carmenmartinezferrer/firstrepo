# Re-introducing myself, a Behind The Data Fashion Brief carousel

Instagram carousel, 1080 x 1350, built to the Behind The Data Fashion Brief carousel format
(hook, story slides, what I do today, honesty slide, CTA, 8 slides max), using the structure
of Mia McGrath's "re-introducing myself" post as the storyline.

The story is Carmen's own, from her message on 1 November 2026. Numbers come from the media kit
skill and her Instagram profile as read the same day (32,679 followers, 207 posts).

Per the DFB workflow this is **text only**. Photos and the Canva build come after the words
are approved.

## Caption

A lil reintro to me, because most of you weren't here in January 🩷

I'm Carmen, Spanish, always lived abroad, now in London. I'm a data analyst in the fashion industry, helping brands use their data to make better decisions, and The Data Fashion Brief is where I talk about fashion through data with an inside fashion tech perspective.

Getting here wasn't linear. I studied journalism, moved into marketing, then finance, did a masters in big data and finally applied all of it to fashion, until I found my dream job. Then I got obsessed with one question: everyone was talking about fashion and nobody was putting the numbers together. So I started writing about it on LinkedIn, soon enough I was writing for fashion magazines, and in January I started my Substack and this account. Ten months later there are 32K of you here, all organic.

My goal is to build this platform outside my 9 to 5, and it's hard, but working on something that's yours is so addictive. The full story is on my Substack, link in bio. What do you actually want to know about working in fashion data? Ask me below 👇

## Design notes

- Your photos only, white Century Gothic overlay, DFB pink #D6447A for exactly one highlighted phrase per slide, "THE DATA FASHION BRIEF" small at the bottom of every slide. Slide 6 is the only illustrated slide.
- Same Canva carousel template as the fashion week learnings post (8 pages, two cover options). Delete the spare cover.
- Mia's trick worth keeping: the cover and the last slide use the same photo, and every slide title is lowercase with one word in your italic or handwritten style.
- No em or en dashes anywhere on the slides, whole numbers only, UK punctuation.
