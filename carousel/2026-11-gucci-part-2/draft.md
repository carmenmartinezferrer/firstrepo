## Slide 1, The sales (reuse part 1's slide 2, change the text)

Small label: **The sales**

**Gucci got Demna, and now it admits the prices went too far**

Demna arrived in July 2025 and the decline narrowed every quarter, but in Q3 2026 sales fell 6% again as the newness wore off, and sales in Gucci's own stores fell 9%. Kering's first answer was to close 125 stores, and now its CEO Luca de Meo has admitted that price rises may have gone further than they needed to.

## Slide 2, The price (reuse part 1's slide 3 layout, two bags instead of four)

Small label: **The price**

**So Gucci cut them, by 30%**

Gucci has quietly started lowering prices on select handbags, and the exact numbers show how far it went. Gucci hasn't announced any of it.

Two price tags, each with its old and new price and the 30% marked:
- **Padlock small bag, France:** EUR 3,500 to EUR 2,450
- **Beatrix large tote, brown suede, US:** $3,300 (October 2025) to $2,310 (November 2026)

Subtitle: "Gucci retail prices, before and after, 30% lower". Each tag says its own market and currency, so France is in euros and the US in dollars.

## Slide 3, The public notices (small label: "The problem")

**The public is starting to notice, and the problem is bigger than Gucci**

Shoppers have started to see it, since carryover styles like T-shirts, sneakers and handbags are reported to have come down by up to 30% in Europe, the US and China. The problem for Louis Vuitton and Gucci is that growth in the mid-price part of the luxury market has evaporated, and both brands make more than half of their sales from middle-class shoppers who spend roughly $2,000 to $3,000 a year on occasional treats like a handbag or a piece of jewellery, according to Bernstein as reported by the Wall Street Journal.

## Slide 4, My take

**Cutting the price of the most expensive pieces won't fix this**

The strategy isn't working because people have cut their spending for economic reasons, and the middle-market shopper who used to stretch to something super luxurious has gone down to mid-price instead. A luxury brand can go one of two ways, either it focuses on the super wealthy and makes them feel something, or it offers real mid-range entry-level prices, and [Carmen: Burberry or something like that, which product and price?] is the kind of example I mean. What I don't think works is lowering the price of the ultra luxury pieces, because the fix has to connect with the customer, and a discount on a $3,000 bag isn't a reason to love the brand again.
