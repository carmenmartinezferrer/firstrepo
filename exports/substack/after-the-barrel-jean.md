# After the barrel jean, the next jean is… a normal one

**Subtitle:** I tagged 997 Paris looks and a year of Google searches to find the barrel's successor. It's the relaxed straight leg, worn with a blazer.

---

The next big jean is not a new shape. It's the relaxed, full-length straight leg, and the runway and the search bar agree on it.

I've spent the last few weeks tagging every look from the Paris and New York Spring/Summer 2027 shows (garment, fit, fabric, colour, all of it) and putting that next to a year of worldwide Google search data, because the question I kept getting asked after fashion month was "so what replaces the barrel?". Everyone was expecting something more dramatic. The data says the opposite: after a few seasons of wide, baggy and curved, denim is settling into a quieter, comfier place. Here's what I found.

## Paris treats denim as a basic, not a statement

Denim showed up in 4 percent of all the looks in Paris, which sounds small until you see that those looks were spread across 63 percent of the houses I analysed. So light in volume, wide in reach. Every single denim look carried exactly one denim piece (no denim-on-denim anywhere in Paris), and denim was the main fabric of only 18 looks, seven of them at Stella McCartney. Everywhere else, the jean was the supporting act.

And it really was the jean. Trousers were 75 percent of the denim pieces, with straight leg and wide leg as the top shapes, and the straight leg concentrated at Maison Margiela, Stella McCartney and Tom Ford. The fit was relaxed on 77 percent of the pieces. If you were waiting for the skinny comeback, there was exactly one skinny jean in the whole of Paris, at Tom Ford. On colour, mid-wash led with 55 percent (mostly Balenciaga and Stella McCartney), followed by indigo (mostly Tom Ford and Miu Miu).

## The blazer is the tell

The styling is where the barrel's successor becomes obvious. Wool suiting appears in 23 percent of the denim looks and leather in 14 percent, so the formula is a blazer worn with jeans, especially at Tom Ford and Maison Margiela. And the pairing isn't random: mid-wash sits on straight trousers about nine times more often than chance would predict.

Tom Ford put indigo jeans under blazers, next to pinstripe jeans, white jeans and denim shorts. Balenciaga went almost entirely mid-wash with shirts and T-shirts, five wide leg and three straight. Stella McCartney led on volume, with denim in 16 percent of her looks, mostly straight jeans plus a denim jacket, a denim shirt and palm-printed white jeans.

The houses doing something genuinely new with denim were the exceptions. Maison Margiela (denim in 14 percent of its looks) showed three denim jackets, a denim maxi skirt and charcoal denim, the only house in Paris to use that wash. Miu Miu did lace-up jeans, a lace-up denim pinafore and crinkled denim skirts, the only denim all season with a finish other than matte. Chanel paired a denim corset with a tulle skirt, and Stella's printed and patchworked jeans carried most of the season's surface decoration. Fun to look at, but not where the volume is.

## New York had already said the same thing

New York pointed the same way a few weeks earlier. Denim appeared in 38 percent of the shows I analysed, most present at Ralph Lauren, Tommy Hilfiger, Calvin Klein, Conner Ives and Thom Browne, and the piece connecting it to Paris is the relaxed denim trouser.

Paris spread denim across more houses (63 percent against New York's 38 percent), but both cities describe the same shift: denim has gone from statement to staple. It's present across a big share of collections without any one house turning it into a season-defining look, and the jean doing the work is relaxed, not skinny.

## Retailers are selling fit, not a shape

This is why designers can afford to treat denim as a basic: the money is in fit, and that's true across several of the biggest denim sellers, not just one.

- **Gap** posted comparable sales growth of 10 percent in the second quarter of fiscal 2026, its eleventh consecutive quarter of positive comps, with management naming denim among its destination categories. Old Navy, which calls itself the third largest denim brand in the US, said it would build on newer silhouettes like low rise and baggy in the second half.
- **Levi Strauss & Co.** raised its full-year outlook in July after second-quarter revenue rose 8 percent, with looser fits among the drivers.
- **American Eagle** saw brand comps fall 1 percent in the second quarter, partly because it was rebalancing women's denim inventory, but reported a good response to new women's fits including a wide-leg straight and a low rise.

None of them is selling one silhouette. The growth comes from a whole family of fits (baggy, wide, straight, low rise, bootcut) sold side by side, which is exactly how people now shop for denim: less as a trend purchase, more as a hunt for the pair that fits and is comfortable every day.

## What people are actually searching for

Search data adds a nuance the runway can't give you: the barrel is maturing, not dying. Worldwide interest in "barrel jeans" peaked in April and has since settled at less than half that peak, but it has stayed higher than the same week a year earlier in every single week of the past year, and over the past two months it has run at almost double last year's level. The barrel stopped accelerating, but the people who found it haven't left.

*[Chart 2: "barrel jeans" search interest, this year vs last]*

Where runway and search really line up is the straight leg. Among the most searched jeans queries worldwide over the past year, "straight jeans" scores about three times higher than "barrel jeans" on the same scale, and the straight-leg queries are also among the fastest growing: "straight jeans" up 130 percent, "straight leg jeans" up 150 percent and "straight fit jeans" up 180 percent against the previous period, ahead of the barrel's 120 percent.

The skinny, meanwhile, hasn't stuck. Interest jumped sharply in the spring, but by early October it was back to last year's level, which matches that single skinny jean in Paris rather neatly.

A few more things from the same list that I didn't expect:

- The most searched jeans query of the year was **"men's jeans"**, and it also grew the fastest of anything on the list, up 700 percent. The denim conversation is not only a womenswear one.
- On colour, "black jeans" and "blue jeans" come next, but the growth is in lighter, warmer shades: "white jeans" up 70 percent, "light blue jeans" up 80 percent, "brown jeans" up 110 percent. That lines up with the mid-wash and white denim Paris showed. The decline sits in earlier cycles, with "mom jeans" down 10 percent and "flared jeans" down 20 percent.
- Brand searches point the same way as the results. "Levi's jeans" and "Calvin Klein jeans" are both up 200 percent, "Gap jeans" up 140 percent, "Old Navy" and "H&M" up 120 percent each, while "Zara jeans" rose a more modest 20 percent. The fastest-growing names are the heritage denim labels, so when someone is looking for the right pair, a known fit from a known denim brand seems to count for more than a new silhouette.

That's the same instinct the Paris runway reflects: 77 percent of denim pieces cut relaxed, the straight leg outgrowing the wide leg three to one, a customer who already made the straight jean the most searched shape, and designers now putting it under a blazer.

## What this means for brands and retailers

The barrel isn't disappearing. Search is still running ahead of last year and retailers are still selling it inside broader fit families. But the runway signal for Spring/Summer 2027 is that the next jean is less extreme, not more: relaxed, full length, straight or gently wide, mid-wash or indigo, styled with tailoring rather than worn as denim-on-denim.

For brands, the opportunity is in fit range and wearability. For retailers, it points to assortments built around the straight leg as the core, with house-specific details (lacing, charcoal washes, prints, even embroidery) as the points of difference.

The real test is when these collections land in stores next year. Before that, the holiday season will tell us whether the demand behind looser, straighter fits holds while the barrel matures. My read: it will, because comfort is a much stickier reason to buy than a shape.
