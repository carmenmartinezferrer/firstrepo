# DFB Reels Formula — Reference Doc

Built from 8 transcripts of consistently-viral creators.

---

## 1. What each video is actually doing

**1. "Guess which one went viral" (egg / restaurant / zero-waste)** Shows two versions of the same content back to back, asks the viewer to guess which performed better, reveals the answer, then names the *exact* technique that made the difference (hook speed, leading with a pain point instead of a generic line, assuming zero prior audience knowledge). Repeats the same beat 3x in one video. This is a teaching format disguised as a game — the prediction is what keeps people watching to the reveal.

**2. Myth correction + framework (minimalism)** "Everyone thinks X, that's wrong" → redefines the term properly → gives the underlying framework (not a look, but a set of tools: mix of fabric, silhouette, finish) → bridges the framework into an adjacent domain (fashion → makeup) → ends on one concrete tip.

**3. Trend listicle (Copenhagen Fashion Week)** Rhetorical-question hook → urgency line (event is next week) → announces the list → runs through items with a one-line styling note each. Solid, but the least differentiated of the eight — it's a competent list, not a mechanism.

**4. "I keep noticing" + research (neurodivergent women)** Names a pattern the creator keeps observing → identifies who's overlooked because of it → cites a study/expert for credibility → adds personal vulnerability → closes on a community line. Built entirely to get people to comment "this is me."

**5. Decode two codes (Georgina Rodríguez / Cecilia Sánchez styling)** Then-vs-now hook → introduces the subject with an authority tag → lays out two contrasting "wardrobes" or codes → decodes each with specific, named details → offers a personal reading with historical logic → lands on one sophisticated closing line.

**6. Historical reframe (women "ruined" the workplace)** Quotes a provocative headline → promises context → drops a surprising historical fact that flips the premise → gives one hard stat → closes with a POV line, sometimes a rhetorical question.

**7 & 8. Data-backed trend reveal (orange trend / fall fashion sentiment)** Personal or relatable hook → thesis ("this isn't random") → chronological evidence build (named dates, brands, events) → one hard stat (+55%, +23%) → zooms out to a broader principle or precedent → lands the conclusion.

---

## 2. The core mechanism, stripped of topic

Across all eight, the engine is the same three moves in different order:

1. **A claim that creates tension** — a myth, a prediction game, a provocative headline, "this isn't random." Tension is what stops the scroll, not the topic.
2. **Proof, not opinion** — a stat, a study, a timeline of named events, a side-by-side comparison. The creator never just asserts; they show receipts.
3. **A reframe that's quotable** — one closing line that hands the viewer language they didn't have before ("two wardrobes, two idioms," "the difference is the edit," "so no, women didn't ruin the workplace").

Formats 6, 7, 8 are the closest match to what DFB already does natively — they're basically unpolished versions of **"I'm a fashion data analyst, here's a shift I'm seeing."** You're not copying someone else's lane, you're tightening the execution of your own.

## 3. Master DFB Formula (primary — use for weekly "Shift I'm Seeing" and "Stop Calling Everything a Trend" Reels)

| Beat | Time | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–3s | Relatable moment, provocative headline, or flat claim that creates tension | No greeting, no "hey guys." First line must work with sound off (on-screen text = hook). |
| **Thesis** | 3–6s | State the POV in one sentence: "this isn't random" / "here's why that's wrong" | This is the line people would screenshot. |
| **Evidence 1** | 6–12s | First concrete data point or named example | Real brand, real date, real number. Specificity is what makes it feel researched, not opinionated. |
| **Evidence 2** | 12–18s | Second data point, building the pattern | Two examples read as a trend; one reads as an anecdote. |
| **Hard stat** | 18–22s | The one number that makes it undeniable | "+X% vs. last year," "Y% share of collections." This is the differentiator vs every other fashion account — you have the number, they have a vibe. |
| **Zoom out** | 22–27s | Connect to a bigger pattern, precedent, or consequence | This is where the "analyst" becomes "strategist" — what does this mean for a brand manager watching? |
| **Close / POV** | 27–30s | One quotable line + optional question to drive comments | End on the line you want quoted in the comments, not a summary. |

**Fill-in template:**

[HOOK — tension in one line, works with sound off]
[THESIS — "this isn't random / here's why that's wrong"]
[EVIDENCE 1 — named brand/event/date]
[EVIDENCE 2 — named brand/event/date]
[STAT — "+X% vs [timeframe]"]
[ZOOM OUT — what this means for the industry / a brand]
[CLOSE — quotable line, optional question]

## 4. Secondary Formula ("Comparison Reveal" — use occasionally, strong for community/engagement spikes)

Adapt format #1 for **Catwalk Decode** or a **Brand Case Study** teaser: show two brands' approach to the same problem (e.g. two brands' response to the same trend), ask viewers to guess which outperformed, reveal, then explain the *mechanism* — not just "this one won" but "this one won because ___." This works because it's a game, and games get replies and shares in a way pure information doesn't.

[SET-UP — "I'm going to show you two brands' [X]. Guess which one drove more [metric]."]
[EXAMPLE A — quick, no commentary]
[EXAMPLE B — quick, no commentary]
["Which did you pick?" — pause beat]
[REVEAL — name the winner]
[MECHANISM — the specific strategic reason, in analyst language]
[CTA — "follow for the data behind the drama" or similar]

## 5. Two example scripts

**Script A — "Shift I'm Seeing" format**

Everyone's calling [TREND] the next quiet luxury. It's not — and the data says something more interesting. Here's why that take is lazy. In [MONTH], [BRAND A] did [SPECIFIC MOVE]. A few weeks later, [BRAND B] followed with [SPECIFIC MOVE]. That's not a coincidence, that's a pattern — two competitors don't make the same bet unless the data already told them to. My tracking shows [SPECIFIC METRIC] up [X]% versus [TIMEFRAME]. For a brand manager, that's the difference between chasing a trend after it peaks and building the strategy while it's still cheap to move. So no — it's not the next quiet luxury. It's [YOUR REFRAME]. Am I wrong?

**Script B — "Comparison Reveal" format**

I'm going to show you how two brands handled the same [SITUATION/TREND]. Guess which one actually moved the needle. [Brand A clip/example — fast, no commentary] [Brand B clip/example — fast, no commentary] Which one did you pick? It's [Brand B]. Not because the product was better — because Brand A led with the product, and Brand B led with the problem it solves. One's an ad. One's a strategy. If you want the breakdown behind why this keeps working, that's the whole job — follow for more.
